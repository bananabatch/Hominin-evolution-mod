# Hominin Evolution — Features

Everything the mod does today, and everything still planned. For Minecraft 1.21.1 on NeoForge 21.1.244.

Optional companions: **Patchouli** (the in-game guide) and **Player Animator 2.0.4+** (every hominin animation). The mod loads without either.

Anything under **Planned** is not in the game yet.

---

## 1. Stages and evolution

You play a hominin, not a person, and you earn your way up the line.

| Stage | Roughly | Band size | In the mod |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ardipithecus | 4.4 mya | 3–5 | Yes — reached only by dying out completely |
| *A. anamensis* | 4.2 mya | 3–5 | Yes — fallback behind Australopithecus afarensis |
| Australopithecus afarensis | 3.3 mya | 4–6 | Yes — the starting stage |
| *H. rudolfensis* | 2.5 mya | 5–9 | Yes — fallback beside habilis |
| Homo habilis | 2.4 mya | 7–12 | Yes |
| *H. ergaster* | 1.95 mya | 9–14 | Yes — fallback behind erectus |
| Homo erectus | 1.9 mya | 12–17 | Yes |
| Homo heidelbergensis | 700 kya | 15–19 | Reachable; its own content is **planned** |
| Homo sapiens | 300 kya | 25–40 | **Planned** |
| Homo neanderthalensis | 400 kya | 18–25 | **Planned** |

**Passing a stage**
- **Required criteria**, all of them. Australopithecus afarensis: notice a stone deposit, survive 3 days.
- **Days to survive:** Australopithecus afarensis 3, habilis 4, erectus 6 (anamensis and rudolfensis 4, ergaster 6).
- **An optional pool**, any 3 of 5. Australopithecus afarensis: climb 8 trees, forage in 3 places, stun a predator, hunt twice with a sharpened stick, drink in 2 places.
- **A milestone** — the invention that defines the stage: striking a flake for Australopithecus afarensis, carrying fire for habilis.
- Fallback species ask less: 1 required criterion and 2 of 4.

**Evolving**
- A cutscene, deep time passing, and your descendants wake somewhere else in the world. The years are counted down on screen from your old kind's age to the new one's - slowly at first, then faster and faster, ticking as they go, while the stars stream past into streaks - until the count slams to a stop on the new age with a flash and a deep note. Then how long it was (*400,000 years later*), the new name written out beneath it, and how far you were moved. Just black, stars and the game's own lettering.
- You lose everything but the guidebook. Each stage has an arrival kit — habilis gets a stick and a flake, or half the time a hammerstone with 3 chert or 6 quartzite.
- Your old band stays in the past; a new one forms around you, and bands of your new species appear nearby while the species you left begins to die out.
- `/hominin become <stage>` runs exactly this sequence — cutscene, move, fresh inventory, new band — for testing.

**What is remembered**
- Evolving takes everything except a name. At the moment you evolve, the member you were closest to — your mate, else whoever thought most of you — is remembered by name and species.
- One grown member of the band that forms around your descendants is called after them, and starts at +2 bond. Nobody in that band knows who the name belonged to.
- The journal keeps the line: one name from every band you have left behind, ten deep. It survives evolution, death and falling back, because it is stored apart from everything else a player carries.

**Dying out, and falling back**
- Lose your whole band and you are penalised. Lose 3 bands and your species dies out.
- You then drop to a **fallback species** rather than ending — anamensis, rudolfensis or ergaster — with a cutscene reading *"You died out as a species. You are not out yet,"* and how far time was rewound (50,000 years for ergaster, 900,000 for anamensis).
- Their road leads back to the stage you fell from. Lose **2** bands there and the line is finished: Ardipithecus, under `homininSuperHardMode`.
- Fallback species also live out there as neighbouring bands in their era, whatever you are.
- `homininExtraEffort` (world setting, off by default) makes them mandatory rungs instead: habilis → ergaster → erectus.

**Losing the whole band**
- A panic attack: the screen goes dark, *"There is nobody left,"* and you come back to yourself about 90 blocks away with a new band around you.
- The hominin you were stays sitting where it happened, head in hands, carrying your name. Come back within 5 blocks and it joins you — taking a name of its own, and your current stage.
- If you are dead or already watching a cutscene, there is no walk: you simply wake with a new band. Cutscenes never stack or queue, and you cannot be hurt during one.

**Your own death**
- You do not respawn as yourself. A cutscene hands you the nearest member of your band and you carry on as them, with what they were carrying - and their name and sex (the journal shows who you are now). Only a grown member can carry on: die with nobody grown left and the children cannot keep the band going alone - they are lost with you, and it counts as a band lost.
- **Nests are their maker's.** A nest belongs to whoever wove it (or laid it down - yours, or thatch bedding you placed): your band each sleep in their own, never in each other's, and you are turned away from theirs - *"That's my nest. Make your own."* - unless you are their mate or your bond with them is 4 or more. Mates share. Children sleep in whichever nest they like. A wild band still shares one nest between all of it; a nest whose maker is nowhere about is anybody's. **Your mate** sleeps in your nest if you have one within 12 blocks, and if you have none, builds theirs right beside you (within 4 blocks, not 9).
- **Night.** Members who finish their nest turn in: they say *"I'm going to sleep. I won't be wandering further."* and go to their nest (or thatch bedding) from dusk instead of full dark, and stay there. Nobody asleep or turned in is ever teleported back to you, however far you wander.
- **Keep watch with me tonight** (H → Danger, one member, bond 4+, not laid up): they stay up until dawn, walking slow circles round you and looking outward; anything that hunts within 24 blocks and they shout, wake you and the band (at most every 30 seconds). They are the ones who see night raiders coming.
- **Living as another.** *Let me live as you for a while* (H → Social, one member, bond 3 and up, not your mate, not pregnant, not in a fight; once every two minutes): you open your eyes where they stood - the same cutscene - with their health, hunger, sex and the places they held in mind; the member becomes the one you were, where you stood, with your name, health, hunger and memories. Your inventory stays with you.
- **A new era.** Evolving is a long time later: every band you knew is gone (their people too, loaded or not), your mind is emptied of places, and the map is clean - new bands of the new age are met as you go.

## 2. The player's body

- **Thirst:** ten drops above the health bar. Drains faster running, in heat and under open sky; slower in cool weather, rain or water. Below 6 you slow; empty costs health.
- **Hunger** is vanilla's, fed by foraging, scavenging and hunting.
- **Carrying limits by stage:** the hotbar only (9) through habilis; erectus gets a second row; heidelbergensis and sapiens three; only Neanderthals carry all 36. Locked slots are greyed out, and anything left in one is dropped.
- **Creative mode:** while you are in creative, your band cannot die of falls - follow you however you fly.
- **Climbing:** any block up to 4 blocks, or a trunk as far as it goes. Climbers pass through leaves and can stand on the canopy. Australopithecus afarensis climbs best, erectus worst.
- **Falling:** any fall damage at all, once, earns the Lucy achievement.
- **Looks by stage.** Each stage is drawn as its own kind: skin, height, and a face built out from the head. Australopithecus afarensis has a heavy brow and a muzzle; habilis a smaller jaw; erectus keeps the brow ridge over a face gone nearly flat - a nose that stands out from it (the first hominin thought to have had one) over a mouth that projects only a little.
- **Threat display (G):** pant-hoot, leap and frighten what is near. Your band joins in; every voice widens the radius and raises the odds. Erectus adds 30% on its own, habilis 10%. Three displays at nothing earns an achievement and a screen flash.
- **Throwing:** sneak-use a rock, a branch or a **hammerstone**. Before erectus it is a wild heave; erectus throws straight and hard. A hammerstone lands half again as hard as a cobble (and +2 more from an aimed throw: 8 against a rock's 4), and to the head it **rings the skull** - a stagger, a 35% chance of a concussion (the concussed stagger, may lose track of you, and a predator is driven off), and once concussed a 20% chance of a brain bleed; a player hit reels (nausea, weakness). But it risks **shattering**: 20% when it hits something, 10% when it hits the ground - gone, with half the time a flake of its stone left behind.
- **Working things out (hold K).** Some things are never told you - a breeding ground, fertile soil. When one is near you get only a feeling: *"You feel like something deeper could be going on here."* Hold K there and you work it out, for free (it is noticing, not inventing) and it goes on your map. Anywhere on your own ground, thinking turns up whatever your ground hides, with how far and which way.
- **Thinking (hold K):** needs 7 shanks of food and 60% health, on a 3-minute cooldown. It is how every recipe is first worked out — and, once something has run from you, how you keep hold of the chase instead. With empty hands it wanders instead: the day, the night, hunger, thirst, the size of the band, the bones you are carrying.
- **Bleeding, in three tiers.** *External* (teeth, flakes) bleeds and stops. *Internal* (spears, hyenas, the big cats) bleeds harder and holds your healing back while it runs. *Catastrophic* is a clock: 60 seconds, and the only way out is 24 water — four drinks, or three full eggshells. Survive and you are lacerated until dawn; get hurt again or eat raw meat before then and it goes bad — an infection that blocks healing for two days, drains hunger and makes you sick. Death by any of it says so: *bled to death*, *bled out*, or *a bleed inside the skull* (never "magic").
- **Ticks.** At most two a day. Past six, you mend only so far until somebody grooms them off you.
- **Healing held back, not stopped.** Whatever is holding your healing back caps it rather than stopping it: ticks let you mend to 8 hearts, blood loss to 6, infection to 5, lacerations to 4 (of 10). The journal says which, and to how many hearts. A wounded animal still does not mend at all while you follow it.
- **Healing:** exactly one thing can ever stop you mending — blood loss, infection, lacerations or ticks. A worse affliction replaces a lesser one and wipes it; a lesser one arriving while something worse runs changes nothing. One cause, one timer, and it always tells you which.

## 3. Tools and materials

**Stone**
- **No mining at all.** Bare hands take only what is soft enough to pull up: leaves, grass, fruit, mushrooms, nests, carcasses, loose rocks. Everything else needs the right tool, and stone stays in the ground until far later. You cannot break a deposit and you cannot put one down.
- Loose surface scatters and outcrops of chert, quartzite and limestone.
- **Basalt** - lava that cooled: dark, heavy, fine-grained cobbles with a rusty weathered top and gas-bubble pits. It lies scattered around every lava pool (and one lava pool in five has a low outcrop of **basalt columns** beside it - a deposit worked with a hammerstone like any other: 2 basalt a strike, hammerstones of basalt, remembered as an outcrop), thick near the lava and thinning out to 40 blocks away (as far as the chunks around the lava reach - 24 to 40 blocks), on the cave floors round underground lava lakes, and in patches on the high peaks where obsidian weathers out, twice as often and spread wider. Every surface lava pool that throws basalt also leaves a piece or two of obsidian right at its edge - so a trail of dark stones means obsidian ahead. Basalt is a tier behind chert: good knapping stone, never flawless in the Acheulean, 10 of it for a multi tool, and +0.25 damage on its tools.
- Outcrops come in three sizes: a low knuckle you could walk past (40%), a proper outcrop up to three blocks high (45%), and now and then a big one - nine blocks across, up to five high, often with a second crest - that you can see from a long way off (15%). Every one is solid rock at the heart, breaking up into scattered stone at the edges, with roots running down under it.
- Two loose cobbles make a hammerstone; a hammerstone opens deposits.
- A worked face gives 2–3 rocks: 22% chance of a hammerstone on quartzite, 18% on chert, 22% of a chert nodule. Loose quartzite gives one 25% of the time. Plain country rock is empty 40% of the time.
- **A seam holds two round cobbles and no more.** It keeps giving stone forever, but once its hammerstones are out it is flat rubble — so hammerstones mean walking to the next outcrop rather than standing at one.
- Limestone will not hold an edge. It is good for grinding stones and nothing else.

**Knapping** (P, holding a rock with a hammerstone in the off hand)
- A menu of what you could try: flake, chopper, multi tool, split the core.
- An Oldowan multi tool costs 8 chert, 10 basalt, 1 chert hammerstone or 2 obsidian, and splits back down into 12 chert. Chert stacks to four, so the cost is counted and paid across your whole pack, not one stack.

**Hand recipes** (P, one thing in each hand)
- Nest; pointy stick (plain or sharpened stick + flake); sharpened spear; chopper; digging stick; wooden club; fire-hardened spear; **twine** (thatch + thatch); **fire drill** (stick + stick).
- Most must be thought of first. Trying one you have not worked out fumbles, and sometimes breaks the material.

**What the tools do**
- Flakes cut, crack bones and wear out.
- Sharpened and pointy sticks hunt; pointy sticks cut.
- Digging stick: foraging succeeds 75% of the time and brings up 1–3 insects at once.
- **The digging stick in the hand.** Carried upright like a staff, the blade planted by the right foot. At anything alive, both hands raise it and stab it down and forward, the body driving it. Digging (dirt, sand, gravel and the rest) and foraging: both hands lift it, drive the blade into the ground a block ahead with the weight over it, then lean back on it to lever the soil up - stroke after stroke while you dig, one stroke per go at the ground when foraging. Band members dig the same way when they forage with one.
- Long branch and club: concussions, fractures, brain bleeds.
- Spears cause the worst bleeding.
- Grinding rock puts an edge back on a worn tool.
- **Thatch** comes from cutting grass, ferns, reeds or dead bush with an edge — a flake, chopper or multi tool. Torn up by hand it gives nothing: the length of the stalk is the whole point.
- **The fire drill** is two sticks spun together, and it is the only route to fire that does not need fire to already exist. Use it on dry ground with room above and it lights one — which counts for the habilis milestone exactly as carrying flame off a lightning strike does. It refuses in the rain and is spent doing it.

**Food and water**
- Foraging soil for grubs, beetles and worms; stripping berry bushes; termite-fishing with a stick, which survives the meal. An empty search says why: other primates or Paranthropus working the ground (by name), a dry day, the dry season, ground your band has lived off for more than three days (it gives less every day after, down to four tenths), a kind that lives off meat now, or bare fingers - or plain bad luck.
- **Termite mounds** are fewer (one to a 48-block cell, six cells in ten; far sparser outside the homeland) and never crowd each other, and they stand taller: four to six blocks, a spire on a stepped base with chimneys. **Fish a mound fifteen times and it is fished out** - the soldiers seal every hole for two days. Your band's fishing counts, and so does a Paranthropus troop camped beside it.
- **Super colonies**: one mound cell in twenty in the homeland holds three great mounds eight to ten blocks high, of redder earth with red vents, round a patch of trampled ground. They never run dry, and every stick comes out with brood as well (1-2 grubs). Building beside one does the colony no harm - the termites eat the building instead: anything of wood or grass (logs, planks, thatch, building branches, anything axe-mined) placed within 30 blocks is eaten, in about a day at the mounds' foot and two and a half at the edge; logs rot a stage at a time (decaying, decayed, gone). Blocks that hold things (chests, racks, fire pits) are left alone. Raw meat on the ground there spoils in half the time.
- Cracking long bones with a flake for marrow; cutting meat into chunks.
- **Eating 3D food.** Food drawn with a 3D model (roots, marrow, grubs, beetles, worms, ticks) is eaten properly in third person: the hand is held up at the mouth, bobbing with each bite - for any 3D food, from any mod. First person keeps vanilla's eating view. Roots are a 3D tuber now, with a snapped stem and hair roots.
- **Drinking:** right-click water with an empty hand, or stand in it. The game's crosshair ignores fluids, so the click is traced again server-side.
- **Eggshells:** hold a sharpened or pointy stick in your off hand and use an egg — you drink it out and keep the shell. An empty shell fills at any water and carries a mouthful.

### Homo erectus

- **Taking things down.** Everything an erectus band builds can be picked back up by hand: the knapping station, the work station, thatch, bedding and building posts. A hominin carcass is butchered with any stone tool.
- **Checklist.** Required: survive 6 days, make an Acheulean tool, spend a night by a lit hearth, **hunt a megafauna animal** (a Pelorovis, or a sabertooth, homotherium, giant hyena or crocodile — the killing blow, your band's, or one you drew first blood on and ran down), and **reach knapping level 2**. Then any 3 of: eat 3 pieces of cooked meat, run down big game you wounded (it dies 30+ seconds after your first hit), walk a scavenger off a kill, range 1,000 blocks from where you began as erectus, trade with another erectus band, grow the band to 14, adopt a moral.
- **Milestone → heidelbergensis:** make a tier 2 or better Acheulean tool from anything but obsidian.
- **Hide** drops from cows and horses. **Knapping station:** 4 sticks in the main hand, a hide in the off hand, press P.
- **The knapping station** is a proper workbench now. Open it and lay out a **hammerstone**, a **bopper** (a bone or long bone) and up to **four rows of stone**; it all stays there when you walk away, and breaking the station drops it. The block shows what is on it: an empty hide mat and anvil, then the hammerstone, the bone and a pile of cobbles that grows as you stock it.
  - **One button** along the top cycles the **industry**: *Oldowan* (flake, chopper, multi tool, grinding stone — hammerstone only, no bone needed) and *Acheulean* (erectus on; needs the bone too). Click what you want to make.
  - The first stone laid out is the one worked. For the Acheulean the side panel shows your **odds of each tier** with that stone at your current skill level, and warns if the hammer or bone is missing.
- **The Acheulean.** 2 of a stone per tool. A **hand axe** (chops wood — logs finally break — and is a heavy weapon) or a **cleaver** (a lasting knife: meat, marrow, thatch — and it chops like a chopper). Both are solid 3D knapped stone in the hand; every tier of a tool looks the same. The **hand axe** is remade: a teardrop biface - a heavy rounded butt with a patch of the cobble's skin left on it, widest a third of the way up, tapering to a point; lenticular in section with a thin, sharp rim all round; scars knapped from both faces, offset face to face, and a median ridge up each face. The cleaver is modelled properly: a rounded butt, sides flaring to one straight, thin, glassy bit across the end, and flake scars and a ridge on both faces.
- **Swinging them.** The hand axe is carried low in front in both hands, lying level. Attacking: a heavy, sluggish sideways slash — dragged out to the right at chest height, a beat while the weight gets moving, then swept across and carried through to the left, the axe level and its edge leading the whole way. Breaking a block, it works like the cleaver's push: the body sits back and turns a little into the stroke, and both hands drive it straight in, level, pointing ahead. Hold the button and it goes again and again from the grip, both hands staying on it; let go and it goes back to the carry. The cleaver (and the chopper) is carried low in front in both hands, flat face down and pointing ahead; to use it, the body sits back a moment, then both hands drive it straight forward with the whole body leaning in behind - it never rises or dips, the blade stays level and face down the whole way - and it comes back to the carry. It lands quickly, well inside the cleaver's own attack time. The carries show in third person; first person holds both tools pointing forward and in toward the middle of the screen, flat. Band members swing them the same way, and lean with their whole body just as a player does. Every other tool or weapon without its own swing slashes like a flake.
- **Stone tools have materials.** Every stone tool - flake, chopper, hammerstone, Lomekwian core, multi tool, grinding stone, hand axe, cleaver - is made of the stone it was knapped from, and looks it: **basalt** (dark grey), **quartzite** (pinkish, grainy), **chert** (honey-brown and waxy, with a chalky white rind on a chopper), **limestone** (pale cream) and **obsidian** (black glass with a violet sheen). Basalt adds 0.25 damage, chert 0.5 (a quarter of a heart), **fine chert 1** (half a heart), obsidian 1.5 - and a quarter of the time an obsidian cut opens one bleeding tier deeper than the blow would on its own (a clean cut starts one; external becomes internal; internal becomes catastrophic). The tooltip says what it is. Tools knapped from plain country rock are nothing in particular and keep the plain look, as do tools made before materials existed. Hammerstones struck from a face are that face's stone. The creative tab has a hand axe, cleaver, chopper and flake of each stone.
- **The flake** is refined: a striking platform at the struck end, the bulb of percussion under it, two ridges on the back meeting in a Y, thin feathered sides and a curve toward the tip.
- **Lomekwian cores** can be made once every two minutes - the hands need a rest between mistakes.
- **No planks.** The vanilla plank recipes are switched off: wood is split and worked, not sawn.
- **Wood.** The hand axe fells trees outright and works anything made of wood — planks, slabs, stairs, fences, gates, doors, trapdoors, signs, ladders, chests, barrels, crafting and work tables. Choppers (the chopper, the cleaver, a Lomekwian core, the Oldowan multi tool) still only hack a long branch off a trunk and leave it standing.
- **The chopper** is modelled properly now: a fist-sized, water-rounded river cobble, tan cortex with a few low swellings, one end struck from both faces into a heavy, sinuous, zig-zag edge with grey flake scars offset face to face. It is carried and swung exactly like the cleaver - carried flat in one hand, caught by the other and driven straight forward.
- **Trade value.** Hand axes and cleavers trade at Treasured (5) when excellent or flawless, Prized (4) at tier 2–3 and Crafted (3) when crude, and better-made ones rank higher within a tier; to bands before erectus they are all Treasured. Hides, twine, thatch, cooked meat and worked wood are tradeable too.
- **A flawless hand axe is the multitool.** There is no separate Acheulean multitool: a tier 0 hand axe carries the `multitools` tag and also counts as a flake and a hammerstone everywhere the game asks, and its tooltip says *Multitool*. The odds panel reminds you when a flawless one is possible.
- **Quality tiers** 4 (crude) to 0 (flawless). Durability ×0.4 / ×0.7 / ×1 / ×1.6 / ×2.5. Damage rises 0.375 per tier: a hand axe hits for 3.5 at tier 4 and 5 when flawless (cleaver 3–4.5). Tiers 4–3 sometimes cause external bleeding, tier 2 internal, tier 1 always internal with a 15% chance of catastrophic, tier 0 30%.
- **Knapping skill** levels 4 to 1, shown in the journal. Leave 4 after 1 tool, 3 after 2 more, 2 after 3 more; thinking (K) while holding chert, obsidian or a good Acheulean tool sometimes counts. A Lomekwian knapper starts at 3. Carries over when you evolve.
  - Level 4 makes tier 4; level 3 tier 3 (20% tier 2); level 2 tier 2 (25% tier 1, 40% with obsidian); level 1 tier 1 (15% tier 0 with chert, 45% with obsidian).
  - The stone caps it: limestone never beats tier 4; quartzite tier 3, or 2 at level 1; basalt tier 1 (excellent, never flawless); chert and obsidian can reach 0; obsidian is never worse than tier 2.
  - Tier 0 earns *The Last Tool You Will Ever Need*.
- **Band members** each have a knapping level (mostly 3–2, rarely 1 or 4; shown in Info). Children start at 4, or learn their minder's level or one below it. Erectus members with a knapping station within 24 blocks make their own hand axes and get better at it.
- **The fire pit.** Fire is kept in a **fire pit** now, not a vanilla campfire: at the work station, three logs along the bottom row and five sticks above (the top middle left empty), nothing in the tool slot. A drill on bare ground only makes a flash of flame again (erectus is told to build a pit).
  - **It starts unlit and empty.** Right-click sticks (any kind), branches (any kind) and thatch or nest grass into it - sneak to put in up to eight at once, 24 at most. Right-click it empty-handed to hear what is in it and whether it would catch; sneak empty-handed to take the last thing back out.
  - **Lighting it.** Work a fire drill on it. It needs **three things in it at least** and **something to take the spark** - thatch, or warm embers - or it will not catch at all, and says why. Then each try is a chance: 30%, +10% per thatch (up to 3), +4% per stick (up to 5), +3% per branch (up to 3), +30% on warm embers (+8% on cold ash), +10% if you know fire, a quarter as likely in rain. A failed try wears the drill out 15% of the time. A **burning torch** lights it far more easily (+40%).
  - **Burning.** Lit, everything in it burns: a stick is a minute, thatch half a minute, a branch two and a half; anything fed in after burns on top, up to 30 minutes banked. Rain burns it four times as fast. Standing in it burns you.
  - **Embers.** Burnt out, it leaves embers that stay warm a whole day (+30% to catch) and then cold ash.
  - **It is a hearth:** meat laid on it cooks (four pieces at a time, shown on the fire), nothing that hunts comes into its light at night while you sit by it, a night by it counts for the erectus checklist, it hardens spears and lights torches - and a fire kept burning on your own ground builds presence (+1 for every ten minutes, up to 3 a day). Old campfire hearths still work.
- **Placed torches and carried light.** Use a burning torch on the top of a block to stand it in the ground, or on a side to wedge it into a wall: it lights the area (level 14) and burns for twice what it had left in the hand, then goes out (rain puts it out sooner). Breaking one gives back a burning torch, half burnt. Held in either hand, a burning torch lights the ground round you as you walk (an invisible light that follows your head). Used at anything that is not a block face - a predator - it is thrown.
- **Torches.** A stick with thatch over the top (thatch top middle, stick under it) and three twine in the slot, at the work station. It does nothing until you light it - at a burning fire pit or campfire, flame, lava, a magma block, or off a burning torch in your other hand. A **Burning Torch** burns down in about ten minutes (three times as fast in rain), and water puts it out (it goes back to an unlit torch). **Throw it** (use): whatever it hits catches fire, and any predator it hits runs - even the fearless ones - a clan hyena's whole clan with it. What dies burning from a torch drops its meat **60% cooked meat, 40% charred meat** (a new, poorer food).
- **The primitive work station.** A **log** in hand and a **hide** in the off hand, press P. It opens a proper 3×3 grid with its own tool slot — nothing is made without the right tool sitting in it.
  - **Workable branch:** a hand axe worn against a log (or a long branch) — 2 from a log, 1 from a branch. A 3D squared branch with the knots cut flush.
  - **Workable shaft:** one workable branch with a **cleaver** in the tool slot — trued straight and even. The stock for stone spears, proper digging sticks and species super-weapons to come.
  - **Worked wood handles like a long branch:** both are carried and swung with the long branch's animations and do what it does — reach, concussions, knocking on trunks, throwing, fuel — but they have durability (48 and 64), and when it runs out they become an ordinary long branch.
  - **Club:** two workable branches down the centre column, a hammerstone in the tool slot.
  - **Building branch:** a workable branch and **any 3 rocks** - the same kind or a mix, any of the mod's rocks and knapping stone, cobblestone, stone or flint - **anywhere in the grid** (shapeless), makes 2. It places as an upright post in a ring of rocks; stack them and the ones above stand on the post below with no rocks of their own.
  - **Thatch block:** a full grid of thatch, 4 twine in the tool slot. Left uncured it rots away on its own over time; right-click it with hide to cure it, and it lasts for good. The binding is only on top and around the top of the sides — twine for raw thatch, a laced hide flap for cured — with straw below and cut stem ends underneath.
  - **Thatch bedding:** 3 hide over a row of thatch, 10 twine in the tool slot — makes 2. Lay two side by side to sleep in; a nest still works, but a nest is what leaves you with ticks. Beds laid together join into one: a bed placed beside another turns to face the same way, the hide runs across the join with no hem, the pillows meet, and a bed joined at its head end gives its pillow up to the longer bed.
  - **A hide roof shelters the wall under it.** An uncured thatch block does not rot if a cured one sits anywhere above it in an unbroken stack of thatch - so cure the top of a wall and everything beneath it holds too.
  - **A proper digging stick:** a branch and a hammerstone side by side, 10 twine in the tool slot.
  - **Cooking rack:** a workable branch and 3 sticks, anywhere in the grid, nothing in the tool slot - makes 1: a forked stick **two blocks tall**, with a twine lashing at the fork. Stand **two** in a line with **one or two blocks** between them and use a workable branch on either (two branches for a two-block gap): the branch lays across the forks as a **spit**, and both racks turn so their forks hold it.
    - **Hang food on it**: right-click the spit with food (sneak: up to 4 on a hook), 3 hooks to each block of spit; empty-handed, take down the nearest. It hangs under the bar, side-on, swaying.
    - **Over a lit fire** (a fire pit, campfire or fire directly under the spit) meat **cooks slowly and evenly** and never falls in the ashes: a meat chunk 20 s, marrow 15 s, hominin meat 30 s, a rib 35 s, anything else as a campfire cooks it. New cooked foods: **Cooked Rib** (7 food, and you keep the long bone), **Roasted Marrow** (5) and **Cooked Hominin Meat** (7 - the band still minds). Left over the fire **five minutes after it is done**, meat chars. Fat drips and smokes while it cooks.
    - **With no fire it is a larder**: whatever hangs there keeps - off the ground, it never turns.
    - Take a rack away and the spit comes down with everything on it (the branches come back). The fire pit and campfires cook the new foods too.
  - **Blueprints** - see *Building*, below. Posts and thatch still place freely anywhere too.

## 4. Scavenging and hunting

- Everything that dies leaves a **carcass** — a 3D ribcage block. Animals also die on their own of exhaustion, disease or old wounds, so the country has bones in it.
- A fresh carcass gives a rib, a long bone or a plain bone, plus 1–2 meat chunks. Eating a rib leaves the bone, and the bone cracks for marrow.
- **Old bone beds** generate in the homeland, about as rare as a chert outcrop: 3–5 bones, 2–3 long bones, 1–2 ribs.
- **Giant carcass:** megafauna leave a giant ribcage block instead of an ordinary carcass. It is far too big to pull apart by hand: **crack it open with a hammerstone** (or anything that counts as one) for 9–14 meat chunks, 3–5 ribs, 4–6 long bones and 4–7 bones, scaled by the season. A clan strips it in four sittings, and you can see the meat going; what is left drops less.
- **Who comes for a kill.** An ordinary carcass may draw a **Crocuta clan** (25%; 25–40% if the kill was yours — clans come for your kills too). A giant carcass draws the **giant hyena** 45% of the time (60% in hard times), otherwise sometimes a clan. Kills register themselves, so scavengers walk straight to them.
- A clan strips a carcass only when nobody is standing within 6 blocks of it; with anyone there, they fight for it instead (see Crocuta, under Animals).
- **Lone hominins** are only ever found at **lone camps** (see *Lone camps, redone*, under Interface), sitting by the dead of the band they lost - never at kills or bone beds any more.
- **Prey bolts:** anything you strike runs — Speed III for 2 seconds, then Speed I for 3 — and every animal within 12 blocks runs with it. Predators, the fearless, and baboons with a troop behind them stand their ground instead. Hominins are never treated as prey.
- **Habilis scavenges on purpose:** the band hunts out carcasses within 24 blocks, strips them, and cracks long bones with a stone for marrow — *"Sweet, a bone!"* Foraging pays 40% less at habilis, so the carcass is the meal.
- **Persistence hunting:** from habilis, think after the animal has run and it is marked for its first run (30 s). Erectus can re-mark whenever the mark fades, with no cooldown, for 4 water a time. A bleeding animal does not heal for 3 minutes. Only for animals big enough to outrun you.
- **Dead trees for grubs.** Right-click a decaying log (empty-handed, or holding anything that is not a block) to pull it open: up to 3 grubs curled in the rot (one time in five, only sawdust), and the log is left hollow - a decayed log, fit for termite fishing. A dead tree has two logs worth cracking - counted over every log within a few blocks, branches and all - and then it has given everything it had. Only 45% of cracked logs come apart hollow (a decayed log); the rest stay as they were. Dead trees also stand a log shorter than before.
- **Persistence hunting is a skill, level 3 to 1.** When big game breaks and runs, you keep it in sight on your own 20% of the time at level 3, 40% at level 2, 60% at level 1; otherwise you are told to hold K and pick its tracks up. Experience hits harder too: +half a heart at level 2, +1 heart at level 1, on any game animal (band members get their own level's bonus). Two runs-down take level 3 to 2, three more take 2 to 1. The journal (J) shows your level.
- **When you lose sight of it**, the band stops chasing blind and waits for you to think. Every few seconds, a member who tracks at least as well as you may find it for you (*"Mira finds the tracks again and points the way"*), and the chase goes on.
- **Megafauna run for distance, not escape.** Broken, a Pelorovis makes one hard run (Speed III for the first 2 seconds, Speed II to 5 seconds), then has to stop and catch its breath: Slowness IV for 10 seconds, standing still - that is your window. Rested, it watches you, and runs again the moment you come within 10 blocks. Every run is a fresh roll to keep it in sight.

## 5. Your band

Band members are hominins of your own species who follow you and look after themselves.

**Everyday life**
- They forage, pick up food and weapons, pull branches from trees, and build their own nests each night.
- They carry nine things — two hands and seven more — and drop the least valuable for something better.
- Their hands follow the moment: a weapon in danger, a digging tool while foraging, a stick for termites, food in the off hand when hungry, and otherwise the most valuable thing they own.
- They eat on their own, chewing with the sound and crumbs of it.
- They go off exploring alone, glowing while away, and come back with what they found.
- They climb trees to escape, climb walls up to 4 blocks when their way is blocked, pass through leaves while climbing, and come down on their own once it is safe.
- They **sleep in the nests they build**, one body to a nest, and get up when the light or the danger changes.
- They swim rather than walk around water, and bathe in rivers on hot days.

**Talking (H)** — grouped by topic (Food, Tools and things, Danger, Social); right-click one first to speak to just them. **Right-click one while holding food** and they take it straight from your hand — fed, bonded, a want settled, or a stray won over — no menu needed. Everything else goes through the menu: right-clicking with anything else only picks them out, and handing things over is *Here, take this* under Tools and things.
- **Food:** Let's forage · I'm hungry
- **Tools and things:** Here, take this (whatever you hold — food, what they asked for, anything for their pack; feeding a stray this way is how you win it over) · Trade · I need an item · Get me… (sticks, branches, nesting material, any of the four stones — and, as they come to think more of you, a flake, hammerstone, pointy stick, digging stick, chopper, spear, club or multi tool. They can only hand over one they actually have.)
- **Danger:** I'm hurt · Let's hunt together · Don't hunt with me · Let's climb a tree / All clear
- **Wants.** Every member wants something - their favourite food, a preferred stone, obsidian, a branch - with an urgency: eating for two or going hungry above all, then an obsession, then having nothing to hold, then a taste. Only the **two most urgent** in the band say so out loud; the rest keep it to themselves (Info still shows it) until one of the two is settled, and a want never asked of you costs nothing when it lapses.
- **Knap me a tool...** (H → Things, on one member you picked out). Erectus members make a hand axe or a cleaver, anyone from habilis a chopper. Their knapping level decides what comes out - a master's hand axe is excellent, and flawless off chert or obsidian - and what they ask: what they want right now, or else their favourite food, 1 to 4 of it by skill (a chopper is always 1). Bond 5 takes one off; **bond 8 and they do it for nothing**. Hand the price over with *Here, take this*; once paid and they have two good stones (one stone for a chopper - they go looking, or you can give them some), they sit and knap for 25 seconds and hand it to you. An unpaid ask lapses after 10 minutes.
- **Friends who are good at things look after you with them.** At bond 6, a good or great tracker sometimes slips away when you are going hungry and brings you meat, and comes along whenever you go after something; a skilled or master knapper who sees you with no real edge makes you a hand axe or cleaver unasked.
- **Social:** Let's stick together today · Groom them · Get these off me · Let's play · Let's share food · Info · Tribe stats · Let's have a child
- **Culture** (erectus on): your people's ways — see below.

**What they say**
- Every kind of moment has a pool of lines (a dozen kinds of news, a dozen more of things said aloud, the crafting announcements, fear, and thoughts), and the chat never repeats a line said for that moment in the last few times.
- A leader never hears the same kind of news twice in a row, back to back; and something everybody notices at once — a carcass full of bones — is announced by whoever saw it first, once every 30 seconds at most, not by all of them.
- Thoughts draw on the season and on the band's own morals, and never repeat the last few you heard.

**Bonding**
- **Picking somebody out stops them.** Right-click any hominin and they stop, turn to face you and wait; press H within five seconds to talk to just them.
- **Trading** is done face to face: pick someone out, press H, choose *Trade*. You see everything they carry and what each thing is worth to them; choose what to offer from your hotbar, then what you want. Worth is measured in their era, droughts cost a tier, and obsidian obsessives will not part with obsidian.
- **Teach** (under H): pick a skill you know and your band watches you — then do it, and everyone watching learns it. Skills that cannot be shown are told instead, and words only get through to some. Children learn what their minder knows as they grow up. Taught members fish termites far more often, crack extra marrow, and groom you better.
- **Grooming pays.** Two ticks come off per session and you keep them as food. Being groomed puts that member in debt, and they come back and groom you in return — nobody can reach their own back. Ask for it with *Get these off me*; anyone in your band will do it. A member with ticks on them can always be groomed again; bond only rises once per ten minutes.
- **Play:** tag or wrestling, only when nothing has attacked recently. Each round of tag gives an extra second of flight the next time adrenaline hits; each round of wrestling gives an extra five seconds of fight. Up to three rounds per species, and it counts for you too. Children play on their own.
- **Band cohesion, 0 to 55.** A new band starts at **35**, with **ten minutes' grace**: small failings (under 5) cost nothing then - unless it is the dry season. In the **dry season** every loss counts 1.4 times over. Gains count 1.4 times over below 40 (the fraction carried to the next) - but **from 40 every gain is harder won**: 0.7 each, and 0.4 each from 48. *Perfect* is 50 and up, 55 the most there is. The slow slip only starts above 45. It rises with sharing food (+1 to 3, once in 10 minutes), grooming (+1, once in 2 minutes), play-wrestling (+1, once in 5 minutes), feasts for the dead and the skull (+3), meeting a need (+2) and keeping a promise (+2); above 40 it slips by 1 a day unless kept up. It falls with deaths (-3), theft in the band (-1), wants left unmet (-1 each, and they add up), needs left unmet (-6), breaking your own rules (-3, -8) and broken promises (-6). Tribe stats and the journal show it with its tier.
  - **10 and under - dire.** One more failing of yours (a need or want ignored, a rule broken) and the band has had enough: they display at you, sometimes the biggest of them beats you (60% of your health), and they drive you out - the band is gone and it counts as a band lost. Misfortunes (a death, a theft) never trigger it.
  - **20 and under - borderline.** No trades at all. Every few minutes someone tells you what they think of how you lead - and what you should have done (the last failing, named).
  - **21 to 29 - tipping.** They tell you what you could be doing to help the band, and turn down half your trades unless you promise to do better (H → Social → *I'll do better* - only while tipping; at 20 and below it has gone past promises): then they trade, and give you a day - cohesion up 3 and no new failings. Keep it: +2. Fail, or slip before the day is out: -6.
  - **30 to 39 - neutral.** Life as usual.
  - **40 to 49 - positive.** One more joins you when you go after something; bond gains come faster (half the time +1 extra); gifts come half as often again; members freeze less and stand to fight more when something comes for them.
  - **50 - perfect.** Everyone treats you as bond 2 at the least; every bond gain is +1; gifts twice as often; two more hunters join you; foraging members find more and more often; and when anything hurts you, every grown member within 32 blocks comes at once.
- **A band that does not trust you falls apart under pressure.** Below neutral, members freeze more and stand to fight less when something comes for them (worse at borderline, worse again at dire), and there is more theft: 1.4× when tipping, 2× borderline, 2.5× dire.
- **Needs, not wants.** At most one at a time for the whole band, said out loud and marked with a glow: a pregnant member needs a proper meal (a rib, a meat chunk, a grub or a termite stick); an injured member needs a good stone to work while they are laid up. Ten minutes to meet it, with a reminder at half time; hand it over with *Here, take this*. Met: +2 cohesion, +2 bond, and the band saw you put them first. Ignored: -6 cohesion and -3 bond - and it counts as a failing. Tribe stats shows the need at the top, and the name finds them.
- **Injured members.** Hurt below half health by something (not you), a member is laid up for a day: 35% slower, no exploring, roaming, quarrying or joining hunts - they stay with the band - and anything that attacks one has every grown member within 24 blocks come for it. They are not idle: every couple of minutes, with a good stone to hand they practise knapping (and get better), and once in a while they lie still and think, and work out a skill they did not have (once per injury) - which they can then pass on. *"… is back on their feet."*
- **Share:** everyone puts food in and it goes to whoever likes it best — bond for all, cohesion for the band, once every 8 minutes. Tastes change: eat something often enough and it may become a favourite, and they will say so.
- **Grooming:** stand beside one for 5 seconds — bond, a little healing, band cohesion, once per member per 10 minutes. Hair is picked through up to habilis; erectus and later clean skin and grit instead. They groom each other unprompted.
- Feeding raises bond: a favourite always does, sometimes by two; anything else about one time in four.
- **Deaths are announced** in chat, like a named pet's, and each one costs the band 3 cohesion (a misfortune, not your failing - it never gets you driven out).
- Favourite foods raise bond. At bond 3 they look after you unasked: food when you are hungry, a better weapon than yours, a hammerstone if you have none, a flake if you have nothing to cut with.

**Habilis and later**
- Each member prefers chert, quartzite or basalt, or none of them; one in five is obsessed with obsidian and will not part with it.
- They break rocks on their own account, avoid limestone, and complain about it when it is all there is.
- They make their own tools, want things and ask you for them, offer trades, and say what they are thinking. No more than **two** of the band have an open request at once — six people asking you for things is a queue, and you stop listening to a queue. From erectus on, a want left to run out costs a point of that member's bond — by then they expect to be heard.
- Obsidian obsessives rush any obsidian they see and talk about it. They will not trade it and cannot be talked out of it, but ask with an open hand and about one time in five they want you to have it anyway.

**Fear and fighting**
- A predator on the band is everyone's business. Anything else you swing at gets the **nearest one or two** — unless you have called a hunt, when they all come. Never against another hominin.
- Nobody chases anything that is already leaving.
- Your blows never hurt them while you hold anything; sneak-attacking is wrestling, which teaches both of you to fight.
- **Adrenaline**, once every 5 minutes: fight (Strength II, Resistance I, worse wounds inflicted), flight (Speed II for 2 seconds then Speed I for 3, up a tree or back to the band), or freeze — and after 3 seconds the band notices and comes for them.

**Fission-fusion**
- From habilis, a band of more than 4 adults splits into parties by day and merges at night. Only your party, and anyone within 10 blocks, comes when you are attacked.

**The dead**
- Every hominin that dies (your band, other bands, Paranthropus) and every chimpanzee or bonobo leaves a **hominin carcass**. Butchered, it gives 2–3 hominin meat, sometimes a rib or long bone, the **brain**, and a 35% chance of the **skull**.
- **Eating hominin meat** without a norm costs 1 cohesion — the band saw — at most once every ten minutes.
- **The norm** (*Our dead stay with us*, in the Culture tab, erectus and later, kept across evolutions): eating your dead is right, and costs nothing. But it binds — every time one of your band dies, you owe a feast: eat hominin meat or a brain with at least one of the band within 16 blocks, within a day (+3 cohesion). Miss it and it is a betrayal: −8 cohesion and −1 bond with everyone.
- **Kuru.** A brain carries it one time in three (*"The brain tastes strange. Sweet, and wrong somehow."* tells you it did; your journal shows it), whoever eats it — you, or whoever at the feast takes the brain when you do not. Incurable, milk included. Day one, trembling and nausea; late on day two, blindness; the morning of day three, death (*"shook, laughed, and died of kuru"*). Meanwhile predators can tell: camp pressure builds far faster. A band member with kuru shakes visibly and dies after two and a half days. Your own death clears it.
- **Hominin Skull.** Use it to hold it out at arm's length and look it in the eye — the pose holds for two seconds — while the screen shows the picture and *"Whatever took you — a predator, another band, disease — you will be avenged."* +3 cohesion, +1 bond with everyone of yours within 8 blocks, once a day. Achievement: *Tuff Pose + Monologue*.

**Culture: your people's ways** (H → Culture, erectus on)
- The screen fits any window: as many morals as there is room for, the rest a scroll (or the ▲ ▼ buttons) away.
- A screen of morals: each with what it does, when it binds, whether it binds *right now*, and a button to adopt it or let it go. The season and days left sit at the top.
- Morals are situational. Most only bind in the times they are about — **hard times** (a dry season or a dry day) or **plenty** (a prosperous season) — and each pays off in one time and costs in another.
  - *Our dead stay with us* — always (see The dead).
  - *It's okay not to share when times are tight* — in hard times: half as many wants, no bond lost when a want runs out; but no food gifts, and only members at bond 3+ hand food over when you ask.
  - *Stealing is wrong when there isn't much to go around* — in hard times: 70% of would-be thieves think better of it, and the rest are caught and made to hand it back (+1 cohesion, the thief −1 bond). Take food from a hungry member's pack yourself and it costs 3 cohesion - a failing of yours.
  - *Stealing is wrong even when we have plenty* — the same, in a prosperous season.
  - *Sharing is always good* — shared meals give 2 bond instead of 1 and come twice as often; an unmet want costs 2 bond instead of 1. Cannot be held with the tight-times rule.
- **Letting go** takes two in-game days, and the moral still binds while it fades (*Keep it* takes it back up). Once gone, it cannot be adopted again for a day.
- **Theft.** Every couple of minutes there is a chance someone in the band steals — the hungriest, taking food, in hard times (35%); anyone, taking food or good stone, otherwise (15%) — more in a dry season (×1.5) and in a band coming apart (see cohesion). Antisocial members are the thief 70% of the time when there is one, and defy the rule against it half the time unless the band holds *Nobody lords it over the rest*. Unchecked, it costs a point of cohesion and the victim says so.
- **Seasons bite.** In a dry season everyone's hunger runs down faster - yours and the band's - and there is more stealing, so rules about food matter. In a good season, a band that has never decided *Sharing is always good* keeps what it finds: half the time a member will not hand you food or give you anything unasked. Members talk about the rules they do not have - sharing in good times, theft in hard ones, bullies nobody stands up to - and point you to *Our ways*.
- **Antisocial members** (from erectus - none before; any left over from older saves settle down): one in ten is born not caring what the band thinks, and more become it - with no ways of its own, the lower the band's cohesion below 30, the more members stop caring (the band is told). They are the thieves, they will not hand you food or things (unless bond 6), will not trade, never give unasked, are always on at you for food, teach the child they mind nothing, and now and then pick a fight - never to the death, but it draws blood, costs a point of cohesion, and 40% of the time lays the victim up. Tribe stats and Info mark them. A band with ways of its own and cohesion 40+ brings them round in time.
  - ***Nobody lords it over the rest*** (a new way, erectus on): reverse dominance. The band stops 70% of fights before they start, catches antisocial thieves like anyone else, makes shunning work far better and brings the antisocial round faster.
  - **Shun them** (H → Social, one member, erectus on): the whole band turns its back. An antisocial member either bends (40%, or 70% under reverse dominance: +2 cohesion) or walks off for good (+1). Shun someone who has done nothing and the band will not do it: -4 cohesion, -4 bond.
- **You are held to it too.** Carry 12+ food while members near you go hungry and the hungriest come and stand in front of you: *"Pass it around."* **Split what I'm holding between you** (H → Food, shown with food in hand) splits the food in your hand between everyone near who is hungry, hungriest first (+1 or 2 cohesion, bond for some). Ignore it for three minutes: -2 cohesion, and again every three minutes. And they keep count: food handed over, things taken from their packs and errands run are taking; anything you hand them, needs and wants met, sharing and passing around are giving back. Take five more than you give and someone tells you so; keep on to eight and it costs 3 cohesion. The debt fades slowly on its own.
- Adopting any moral counts for the erectus *adopt a moral* goal. Tribe stats lists your morals and the season.

**Mates and children**
- From habilis on, hominins **pair-bond**: one mate, kept. Before that, nobody is anybody's in particular.
- **Nobody arrives paired.** A new band starts single; each member takes one to three days before looking for a mate, and about a third never pair up on their own.
- Well-fed members (hunger above 15, mostly healthy, not bleeding) pair up on their own and announce it in chat, then conceive now and then: *"Asha is expecting. Ido is the father."*
- **You can be chosen too.** Groom or feed an adult of the other sex in your band. It takes three times, or once with a favourite food. Or, at bond 2, pick them out and ask outright: H → Social → *Be my mate*. Then *Let's have a child*: if you are female, you carry it; if male, they do.
- From habilis, **your mate keeps close** (within about 4 blocks, not the band's loose 10). When a predator hits you, your mate gets Speed I and Strength I for 5 seconds; when one hits your mate, you do. At most once every 3 seconds.
- **Pregnancy** takes a day. Hunger runs twice as fast (for you, faster exhaustion); pregnant members ask for food more often and forage more.
- **Labour**, the last quarter: a member goes off alone to a quiet spot, glowing, and predators come for her, with more arriving while it lasts. You glow in your own. While a birth is being guarded, anyone within 48 blocks gets Strength, and your threat display drives off **anything**, the fearless included.
- H → Social → **Tribe stats** (whole band): cohesion, your mate, your morals, the season, days on this ground, your own skill levels; then **Most valuable** (the skilled and master knappers and good trackers, best first), **Asking you for** (the wants said out loud, and tools being made for you), **Expecting** (who is pregnant or giving birth, and the father), **Children** (and who minds them), and **Everyone** (bond, knapping and hunting levels, mate, closest friend). **Click any name** and that member glows for 15 seconds and you are told how far and which way. Scrolls for big bands.

**Family**
- Feed a male and female together and a child comes a day later, grown two days after that.
- Adults mind children; a hurt child brings the band running, a dead one shakes it.
- When a member does something one of your tasks asks for, there is a 40% chance it counts. One-off tasks are always yours.

## 6. Other bands

- **Every band has a name and ground of its own** - 105 blocks round its camp from erectus on (210 across), 64 before - named for what is there: *the River band*, *the Black Stone people*, *the Thorn Tree ones*. Bands are remembered by the world whether anyone is near or not: their people are only about when someone is (within 150 blocks), and come back as many as they were. A band needs a reason to settle - water, food (termite mounds, berries, a carcass) or good stone - and the more of them a place has, the likelier; nowhere without any. **A new band's ground never runs into another band's, or into the ground your band is living on** (so bands settle 136+ blocks off before erectus, 218+ after). No more than three camps within 360 blocks. **They see you coming**: a band you do not know yet knows you are there once you are within 150 blocks of its camp. **In desperate times** a hungry band (desperation 3+) patrols a ring 45 blocks wide outside its ground as well, and catches you there as if you were on it.
- **Calls.** Bands call across the country. A call says who (or *Another band is calling* the first time - and then you know them), how far and which way, and **[Lead me there]** sets a pointer at the top of the screen that turns to face them and counts down the distance, following them if they move, until you arrive. Paranthropus call too.
- **Naming your own band.** When a band forms round you, you name it (a name from your ground is suggested). `/hominin nameband <name>` renames it. Tribe stats, the journal and the map show it.
- They will not join you, but they trade. Value is era-relative: a Lomekwian core is Treasured to Australopithecus afarensis and Common to erectus.
- **Standing with each band, 0 to 50**, like cohesion. Every band starts at 30 - unless it watched you taking from its ground before it ever met you: 3 less for each time, down to 18. Taking water, food or stone from their ground costs 1 (if they saw, or found the signs); striking one of them costs 8 (Paranthropus 5); fighting off their raiders 3. Trades (2, or 3 for something worth having), gifts, travelling together (2) and killing a predator beside them (3) earn it - up to 12 a day per band. **Gifts** let you choose how many of each thing (click +1, right-click -1, shift-click all or none); every piece of food counts 2 (3 for a proper meal), so the more food, the more standing.
  - **10 and under - hostile:** they raid you when things go badly - at night, in hard times, or when your band is thin.
  - **20 and under - unfriendly:** catch you on their ground and they come straight at you with a display: leave, or pay (4 food or 2 good stone), within 30 seconds - or they drive you off with blows until you do.
  - **35 to 44 - friendly:** welcome on their ground - their water and stone are yours to use - except in hard times, when only friends of 40 and up are. They use yours in turn. They will travel with you.
  - **45 and up - allies:** they stand with you against other bands' raids, join you when you go after big game on their ground, come when something hunts you there, and now and then one brings you something.
- **Travelling together has to be earned:** from erectus on only friendly bands (35+) will walk with you; before, anyone who is not against you (30+). In hard times, 40+.
- **The others** (H): every band you know of, near or far, the arrows cycling through them - their kind and size, standing and what it means, where their camp is, whether you are on their ground and may use it - with **Lead me there**, and when some of them are close, **Trade**, **Ask them to travel with us**, **Pay them** (when they are demanding), and **Offer a gift...**: pick anything you or your band near you carries, and it is handed over and weighed. With nobody near to talk to, H still opens the talk menu (The others, and the developer tab) - and Done on The others goes back to it. Near a band that is not your friend (under 35): **Demand tribute** and **Raid them** (asks twice).
- **Their own presence and cohesion.** Every band has a presence (0 to 20, like yours) and a cohesion (0 to 50) of its own, drifting a little every day - presence round a mean of its own (bigger bands higher), cohesion following it with a temper of its own; hard seasons wear both down. A band with presence 11+ keeps predators off its ground (up to 60% of predator spawns there never happen) - and your own ground does the same at presence 11+. The others and the map show both.
- **Tribute.** Once a day per band: your odds are 20%, plus the gap between your presence and theirs, plus 5% for each adult more than they have, plus how far their cohesion is below 25. They pay in food (more the weaker they are): presence +2, standing -6, their presence and cohesion -2. They refuse: standing -4, and a band that holds together may close ranks and stare you down.
- **Raiding them.** Your band falls on theirs (standing -15). They break only on **heavy losses** - their dead, not their bruises: **1** killed for Australopithecus (and Paranthropus, Ardipithecus), **3** for habilis and rudolfensis, **5** for erectus and every kind after (a band with fewer to lose runs when it is down to one; a haven's people fight to the last). Broken, they flee home dropping up to 8 food, presence +3, and theirs drops. If they hold for a minute your band pulls back (presence -2).
- **Voices.** Other bands' people talk when you are within 40 blocks - about you, to you, or quarrelling among themselves when their cohesion is low - and their names are coloured by standing: **blue** allies, **green** friends, grey strangers, **red** unfriendly, dark red hostile. Heard from further than 12 blocks, the line says how far and which way. Your own band talk to you too, every few minutes, by their bond with you: cold, easy, warm, or devoted.
- **Desperation, 1 to 5,** for every band: each day it climbs a step in the dry season (and another on a dry day, and another if the band is coming apart) and falls a step in the rains (and on a prosperous day, and often if they are strong). A desperate band will not trade (30% refusal per step past 2), its people carry less food, it brings you less, it raids more (up to 2.1 times as often), and allies stop bringing gifts at 4. Shown in The others (a meter), on the map and on the encounter screen. Your own band has one too (the season, the day, how hungry they are, whether the ground under you is picked clean), in the journal and tribe stats.
- **Designs on your ground.** Once a minute every band you know within 320 blocks of your camp may come about your ground - more the higher its pressure, more the more desperate they are, less the stronger your presence (14+ halves it, 18+ cuts it to a third, and at 5 or under they come 1.4 times as often - but a band at desperation 4+ is only put off by 30% at most). No more than one every 20-40 minutes, and each band no more than every two days. Nothing happens on ground under pressure 3 unless they are desperate.
  - *Friends and allies* offer to pay for the use of your ground (pressure 4+): accept and you get their offer (food, and stone from a comfortable band on rich ground), they forage there for five days and will not raid or claim it (standing +3); decline, standing -2.
  - *Unfriendly and hostile* bands demand payment (2 + half the pressure + their desperation, in food, or half as much good stone) - or, on pressure 6+ and stronger than you, want the ground itself.
  - *Neutral* bands pick one of the three - offer, demand or claim, weighted by how desperate they are - and keep to it with you until it fails (declined, or fought off), when they try the next.
  - **They walk up to you** - envoys walk over from their people or come over the horizon - and when one is within 8 blocks **a screen opens**: their name in the colour of your standing, their desperation, what they say, what they offer or want. **Accept / Decline** an offer; to a threat **Give in** (pay; or, to a claim, pack up and leave them the ground), **Fight** (they come at you and your band stands with you - break them and they give up; hold out a minute and they take food, and on a claim, your ground), or **Flee** (you drop half of up to four stacks - all your food and some other things - to the nearest of them, get Speed II for 12 seconds, and they chase you, hitting whoever they catch; on a claim of ground worth 5+, they take the ground). The answers are in chat too, as links (`/hominin answer`), if the screen is shut. Raids for food, and being caught on a band's ground, use the same screen now.
  - **A desperate band warns you first:** *"Someone hungry has decided a different route to provide."* with how far and which way.
  - **Hiding.** Pack up, or be more than 48 blocks from camp, when a demand or claim arrives and they go to your camp instead: they take everything lying on the ground within 16 blocks and half of what is on a knapping station - and if you packed up and the ground's pressure is 6+, they move onto it.
  - **Help.** A friend or ally at desperation 3+ may come asking you to join a raid on a weaker band they are not friends with you: *Join them* (two minutes later, the raid works or does not - their and your presence against the target's, and its cohesion: success gives you 3-6 food and maybe chert, ally +4, target -10, presence +2, their desperation -2), *Share food* (4 food from you and your band: their desperation -2, standing +3, and nobody needs raiding), or *Decline* (-1).
  - **Taking ground.** Break a band in your own raid and for a day their ground is yours to take: set your territory on it and they move 300 blocks off (presence +3). Your band speaks up about it - when it is desperate (3+), or strong (presence 14+) on poor ground (3 or less) - naming the weaker band with better ground and what it holds, with a link to lead you there.
  - **Allies lend you presence:** +1 a day for each allied band within 400 blocks (2 at most).
- **Desperate times.** When at least three bands hold ground and 60% or more of them are at desperation 4+, desperate times begin (announced to everyone), until fewer than 35% are. Every band's desperation climbs a step more each day (never below 2); raids for food come twice as often, demands and claims on your ground 1.6 times as often, night raids twice as often; and **patrols**: any band that is not your friend and is at desperation 3+ watches its ground, catching you from 40 blocks rather than 24. The journal says when it is on.
- **Bands have allies.** A new band has a 35% chance to take the nearest band within 300 blocks as an ally, and a band with none finds one 5% of days. Raid a band and every ally of it you know drops 8 standing. A band coming for you - a food raid, a demand or a claim on your ground, a night raid - may bring its allies (2 more of theirs each): 25% per ally, plus 8% for every point of your name, up to 80%. They fight alongside if it comes to blows and go home with the rest.
- **Your name, 0 to 10.** Breaking a band in your raid +2, fighting off raiders +1, a joint raid that works +1; it fades a point every other day. Every point makes raids, demands, claims and night raids on you less likely (a name of 10 cuts them to a quarter) - and makes allies more likely to gang up on you. Shown in the journal.
- **Night raids** (erectus on): once a night at most, a known band at desperation 4+ (3+ in desperate times) that is not your friend may come in the dark (5% a minute per step of desperation past 2, less for your presence and name). If someone is **keeping watch** - or you are still awake - they are seen coming: the watcher shouts, you and the band are woken, and they walk in and demand food - give in, fight or flee (the band's allies may be with them). If nobody is watching and you are asleep, they are in and out: up to 6 food from your band's packs, and half the time someone hurt (never killed); presence -2.
- **Bands die.** Once a day each band out there may not see the next: 1.2% at the least, more for a desperate band (+1.5% a step past 3), one coming apart (+2% under cohesion 10), a weak one (+1% under presence 10), less for a strong one; twice as likely in desperate times. Hunger, sickness, hyenas in the night - word comes to everyone who knew them, and what they knew of the country goes with them. From erectus, their tools lie where their camp was: an **old tool deposit**. A band whose last member is killed goes the same way.
- **An ally in trouble.** When it is a band you stand with (45+) and you are in the world, it does not simply die: its alarm reaches you - a horn, *"Screaming on the wind"*, how far and which way, and the pointer set on their camp - and you have **150 seconds** to get within 40 blocks. Then it is a fight at their camp (a hyena clan, a giant hyena, or a great cat, going for their people): drive the attackers off or kill them within 100 seconds and they live - standing +8, presence +3, your name +1, their desperation down and cohesion up, and the **When It Was Them** advancement. Too late, or they are overrun, and they are gone.
- **Allies come to you.** When your band is going down - a fight has taken it to 60% or less of the grown people it had at its best today (with at least 3 then), and something is still attacking you or yours - your allies within 500 blocks hear it (up to two bands): *"...heard the fighting - they are coming!"* **Ten seconds later** three of each are there, beside you, fighting whatever is fighting you. They stay up to a minute, then go home. At most once every ten minutes.
- **Allies come to trade.** An ally sometimes sends somebody over with things to trade: the screen shows what they carry - *See their offer* opens the ordinary trade screen with them, where you pick what you want and what you give; *Not now* costs nothing.
- **Talking to the other band.** With your band and another both near, the H menu's title is clickable: *Talk to your band* becomes *Talk to the other band*, and back.
- **Raids.** A hostile band - or an unfriendly one, or in hard times any band not friendly to you - may come for your food, no more than once a day and a half, and the softer a target you look the likelier: low presence and low cohesion both count, a band that is coming apart takes more chances, and a strong one is surer of itself. The raiders walk in and demand food: pay (the link in chat, or The others), or fight - they break only on heavy losses, the same 1 / 3 / 5 dead as above (they run, presence +3, standing -3) - or do nothing and they take 4 food from your band and you, and the band saw you let them (cohesion -2). An allied band near you turns raiders back.
- **Afraid of you.** Break a band on heavy losses (your raid, or theirs on you) and half the time they are **cowed**: they leave you be - no patrols on their ground, no demands, no raids, no claims - until they are as many as they were, hold a haven (the best armed there are), or five days have passed and they have armed themselves again.
- **A name for killing.** Killing other bands' people when nobody forced it on you - not in a raid on you, a fight they brought, or one who was coming at you or yours - is remembered: +1 a death, +4 for the last of a band. At **3** word gets about: every band you meet after starts lower with you (down to 11, 2 less for each point) and says it has heard; one that is no bigger than your band is **afraid of you** 40% of the time, and keeps out of your way as above. At **10** the bands round about are afraid enough to come together: now and then (at most every three days) the two or three nearest that are not your friends **raid you together** - they walk in and it is a fight, no talk; their dead all count toward breaking them. It is forgotten when you evolve.
- **By season:** in hard times (a dry season or a dry day) trades need a tier more, and walking up to their camp gets you bared teeth and *"Keep walking."* - unless they think well of you (40+). In a prosperous season trades need a tier less, and someone comes over and hands you food - once per band per season, if you are not in their bad books.
- **Paranthropus holds no ground**: a troop only settles where there is food or water, and moves on every day, 80-160 blocks, to somewhere with more - and in hard times, off every band's ground, because the bands drive them off (you are told, if you know them). Known troops are marked on the map where they are now.
- Species come and go: Australopithecus afarensis is gone by erectus, habilis thins out, erectus lasts until sapiens.
- **Paranthropus boisei**, from Australopithecus afarensis until antecessor: troops of 3–6, dark and heavy-built, with a crest along the skull, a broad flat face and flared cheekbones. Not a stage you can play. They carry long branches and sharpened sticks, forage constantly, and **strip the ground for 60 blocks** — foraging near them works about a third as often, and in a drought barely at all. A threat display, or hitting one with anything (a branch, a thrown stone), sends the troop off 100 blocks, taking their foraging with them. They never fight you over it. Killing one leaves a hominin carcass. They are also good neighbours: when a predator comes into the country within 128 blocks of a troop, they shriek, and you are told which way it is coming from — once per minute at most, so a pair of cats is one alarm. Under H they only understand three things: *Trade*, where about 40% of lowball offers up to two tiers short get through (*"You got the better of that one"*); *Show me good stone*, where one walks you to the nearest chert or quartzite, waiting when you fall behind; and *Show me obsidian*. They guide once a day per troop. Make an Oldowan tool in front of them and the ones watching learn it, and knap now and then from then on; the rest only rarely pick it up once Homo habilis is about.
- While another band is travelling with you, **your own** band glows, so you can tell yours from the guests.
- Guests go home at dusk — and now go on their own if their alpha is dead or gone, rather than standing in your camp all night.

## 7. Animals

- **Papio angusticeps:** troops of 14–20. They trade, mob whatever attacks one of them, and their bites bleed.
- **Baboons climb:** up a trunk to sleep in the branches at night, up when frightened, and now and then by day for the view. Chimpanzees and bonobos do the same, and chimps go up when a predator comes within 10 blocks.
- **Other primates compete for food:** foraging within 35 blocks of baboons, chimpanzees, bonobos or Dinopithecus works about a third as often, and says why.
- **Baboons climb walls.** Blocked and still trying to get somewhere - after you, away from something, back to the troop - a baboon hauls itself up the face the way a hominin does, up to 4 blocks, and lands without hurting itself. A pit deeper than that holds one as well as it holds you.
- **Baboon alarm calls.** A troop leader that spots a predator within 28 blocks barks the alarm, and the whole troop turns to look. Anyone within 48 blocks hears which animal it is and which way to look (*"The baboons are barking alarm: Saber-toothed Cat, to the north-east."*). Once every 45 seconds at most.
- **Chimpanzees** live in communities of 4–7: often in jungle, fairly often in savanna within 90 blocks of a jungle, and rarely further out from erectus on. They hold **ground of their own - 50 blocks round (100 across)**, shown brown on the map once you have come across it. A stranger on it is warned (hooting, drumming, a chat line); one who is still there eight seconds later and has come further in (within 28 blocks) has the whole community on them. A community that trusts you lets you pass. The alpha also status-checks anyone inside its range — walks up, stands too close, bristles and grunts, with no other warning — and you have five seconds to hand it something or hold K. Pass and trust rises; fail and the alpha makes its point, and trust falls. Hitting one, or displaying at them, opens the same window as a baboon troop. Feed them (sneak-use shows trust) and a community that trusts you grooms your ticks and mobs any predator chasing you through its range. A grudge means attack on sight inside the range.
- **Baboon trust.** Trades and gifts build it; gifts count double. Once a troop trusts you, up to three travel with your band by day, mob any predator that comes near you, and go home at night.
- **The five-second window.** Hit a baboon near its troop and the whole troop goes still and stares while your view narrows. Hand one anything, or hold K to make yourself small, and they let it go. Do nothing — or hit one again — and the troop erupts and holds a grudge: they won't chase you past 24 blocks, but they attack whenever you come within 9. A grudge can be worn down, slowly, with gifts.
- **Bonobos** have their own model: small round head with the hair parted down the middle, flat black face, pale lips, bare dark chest, long thin limbs. From erectus on, in troops of 6–10 by rivers and at forest edges. **Nothing hunts within 64 blocks of a peaceful troop**: no predators spawn, no visitors come to camp, and camp pressure drains away. It's the only safe ground in the game. They give food to anyone nearby who is hungry, groom anyone with ticks without needing trust first, drift along beside you while you forage, and share out whatever food you hand them. Sneak-use tells you where you stand. **Hurt one** (you, or one of your band), and that troop is no refuge any more. Nor is any troop you meet afterwards, for the rest of your line: they keep away from you. There is no apology.
- **Crocodiles** lie in warm water at least two blocks deep: savanna pools, rivers, jungle, swamp. They're twice as likely during a drought. Come within about 7 blocks of the water and one lunges. If it connects, it grabs you and rolls: you're dragged toward deep water, slowed right down, and bitten every second, and the first bite bleeds. One hit of 3 damage or more makes it let go, and so does a band member hitting it. A miss sends it back under to wait. It never chases far up the bank, a threat display does nothing to it, and it drops 2–4 meat.
- **Dinopithecus**, the giant baboon: groups of 2–3, never hunting you, but deadly if you come within 5 blocks. It pauses between bites and never backs off. A threat display does not frighten it — it charges you and earns *Nice Try, Genius*. Its bites cause internal bleeding, sometimes catastrophic. Gone by erectus.
- **Pelorovis**, the giant buffalo — **megafauna**. Herds of 3–5 on open homeland grass, 80 health, horns two metres across. It never starts a fight; strike a healthy one and it turns and charges (8 damage, heavy knockback, sometimes a bleeding gore), and the whole herd comes with it. Worn below 45% it breaks and runs - a burst for distance, then ten seconds catching its breath - and the hunt becomes a persistence hunt. Drops 5–8 beef, 2–3 hides and 1–3 long bones, and leaves a large carcass. Megafauna (tag `hominin_evolution:megafauna`): Pelorovis, sabertooth, homotherium, Pachycrocuta, crocodile.
- **The later megafauna**, from erectus on, grazing near water (within 14 blocks), and staying away from ground you have lived on for more than three days - except near a **breeding ground** (hidden; one region in three of 384 blocks has one, on open grass): within 160 blocks of one they come two and a half times as often, near it, whether or not the ground is worn out. All megafauna: they never start a fight; worn down they break and run and it becomes a persistence hunt; they leave a giant carcass. Their skulls are too heavy to be concussed - a club only breaks their bones (no stun either).
  - **Mammuthus subplanifrons**, the first mammoth: families of 1-3. 160 health, 12 damage, heavy knockback, armour 4, steps up a block. A great domed head held above the shoulders, a trunk that sways and curls at the tip, tusks that go down and then sweep forward and up. Struck while strong (above 35%), it **winds up**: head and trunk go up, it trumpets and stamps for a second and a half - then charges straight at where you were; a tusk strike is internal bleeding a third of the time. After a charge it stands and blows for two and a half seconds: the moment to hit it. One charge in five comes with no warning. Drops 9-14 beef, 3-5 hide, 3-5 long bones.
  - **Megalotragus**, a hartebeest the size of a buffalo: 2-3 together. 70 health, 7 damage, quick. High shoulders, a sloping back, a long face with a dark blaze, horns swept back from a raised base. Struck above half health it snorts, drops its head and hooks - a short, fast lunge (warning 0.8 s; three times in ten none). Drops 4-7 beef, 2-3 hide.
  - **Rusingoryx**, the antelope with a hollow dome over its nose: herds of 2-4. 40 health, fast. It never fights: struck, it honks - deep and carrying - and the herd goes. The persistence hunter's animal. Drops 3-5 mutton.
  - **Bigger.** All three are drawn and sized as big as they really were: the mammoth's model at 2.3x (was 1.6) - over 4 blocks at the shoulder, hitbox 2.9 by 4.5 - Megalotragus at 1.4x (was 1.0; hitbox 1.7 by 2.5), Rusingoryx at 1.3x (was 1.0; hitbox 1.3 by 1.8).
  - **Thick hide**: Mammuthus and Megalotragus take a quarter of the hurt from anything but a fire-hardened spear, a hand axe or a cleaver (item tag `big_game_weapons`), and say so.
- **Pachycrocuta, the giant hyena** — a loner, and much bigger now (70 health, 9 damage, armour, heavy knockback resistance, 1.5× the old size). Rare in the country and a rare camp visitor.
  - **Kill thief.** It goes for the nearest giant carcass within 56 blocks first, then any fresh kill. Anybody standing within 7 blocks of its meal gets attacked outright — no warning — and it eats the carcass whole once it is clear (then leaves everyone alone for half a day).
  - **Stalker.** Between meals it follows the band at 16 blocks, preferring children and stragglers over you, and freezes whenever the leader looks at it. Unwatched for 5 seconds, it goes in; once it has killed one, it runs.
  - **Hard to move:** a display drives it off 70% of the time if you are looking right at it, otherwise 20% + 8% per band member (max 55%) — and when it fails, it growls and stays. It breaks off below 25% health. Driving it off a meal counts as walking a scavenger off a kill.
- **Crocuta, the clan hyena** — the spotted hyena's early line. Clans of 3–5 roam the grass (4–6 in hard times at a kill); 22 health, 3 damage, bites less often (a pass every 2.5 seconds, backing off between) and bleed one time in five. Spawn egg puts down a clan of five.
  - **Neutral until you contest their food.** Come within 9 blocks of a carcass a clan is on and the whole clan barks — the warning. After 4 seconds, if you (or one of your band) are still within 7 blocks, they all attack. Back off past 13 and they settle; they chase you up to 22 blocks from the kill and then go back to it.
  - **G is a bluff.** A display at a clan that is on a kill or after you scatters it (they leave the kill for 45 seconds) with 30% + 10% per band member, max 80%, 10% less in hard times. If it fails, they call it and attack at once.
  - **Morale.** A clan fights for food, not to the death: kill one and the whole clan breaks and runs, and your band stops chasing them. Any one below 35% health runs. A thrown torch scatters the lot. Killing one on a kill, or scattering a clan off one, counts as walking a scavenger off a kill.
- **Sabertooth:** attacks anything within 7 blocks, hunts baboons from 16, and is afraid of nothing — no threat display will move it.
- **Homotherium:** the scimitar cat. Daylight, open country, usually in pairs, faster than you, and it calls its partner in.
- **Crowned eagle:** circles by day and stoops on any child more than 7 blocks from an adult. Losing one earns The Taung Child.
- **Predator pressure:** camping in one place builds it — faster at night, with carcasses about, or while hurt; slower with a big band. First tracks you did not make, then visitors. Moving camp 64 blocks resets it.
- **Your ground.** It is set where your band first wakes (a new band, a new body) and **stays there**. 150 blocks from it you are warned you are too far; past 250 the band settles wherever you are - new ground. **Pack up** on the map (J; it asks twice) and you have no ground: your camp drifts with you as before, and nothing builds presence anywhere, until you press **Set territory here**. Making camp on another band's ground costs 5 standing unless you are allies. On the map, hovering over any band's ground shows its standing, presence and cohesion; your own shows your presence and cohesion. `/hominin territory pack|set` does the same.
- **Nomadism: the home range.** Packed up, your camp - the heart of your ground - drifts to wherever you spend your time. After four days on the same ground (within 200 blocks) you are warned; at four and a half everything that hunts there knows your band: camp pressure builds twice as fast, and every few minutes the country tests you - a predator comes calling, something steals food out of a band member's pack (presence -2), or one waits for whoever strays furthest - more of them and sooner every day you stay, with a second visitor as well after six days. Move 200 blocks and it starts over (*"New country. Nothing here knows your band yet."*). Bonobo ground stays safe.
- **Presence, 0 to 20**: how well the country around your camp knows to leave your band alone. New ground starts at 8. A predator killed by you or yours +2 (+4 for a sabertooth, homotherium, giant hyena or crocodile), a kill taken off a hyena clan +3 (+4 off the giant hyena), raiders fought off +3, a fire kept on your ground +1 per five minutes (5 a day), building on your ground +1 per three fire pits, thatch, bedding, posts or stations placed (4 a day), a settled band of four or more +1 a day, allies within 400 blocks +1 a day each (2 at most), tribute +2, breaking another band +3; food stolen from the camp -2; it fades 1 every other day above 12. Old saves are brought down to the new scale once.
  - **0 to 5 - weak.** Other bands raid you half as often again, and any band that is not your friend may try it, hard times or not; predators hardly ever let you pass; and allies hang back half the time instead of standing with you (*"...your band is nothing on this ground, and they hang back."*).
  - **6 to 13 - ordinary.** From 11, predators start to stay off your ground (up to six in ten never spawn there).
  - **14 to 17 - strong.** Half of the country's tests are turned away (*"Something came to test the camp, saw whose it was, and thought better of it."*), demands on your ground come half as often, and nothing raids a food pile left out at night.
  - **18 to 20 - commanding.** Seven tests in ten turned away; demands a third as often.
  - High presence also slows camp pressure building. But presence never stops the ground wearing out: foraging gives less after three days, and **the big herds - Pelorovis and the later megafauna - stop coming to ground you have lived on for more than three days**. Tribe stats and the journal show your presence.
- **Standing by stage:** australopithecines are prey; armed habilis with 3 band members nearby gives predators pause; armed erectus is not attacked at all.
- **Predators are not monsters.** One takes two of the band and then leaves with what it came for — *"The sabertooth has what it came for, and goes."* It keeps that count until it dies, so one that finds a new target cannot start again and work through everybody. Satiation is not fear, so even the fearless walk away from a full belly.
- **Predator damage** (before difficulty): giant hyena 9, sabertooth 7, homotherium 6, clan hyena 3, Dinopithecus 5, crocodile 4 a bite, chimpanzee 4, crowned eagle 4 — they hurt, and they open wounds, but no single bite kills a healthy hominin.
- **Predators fight in passes.** A bite, then a real pause and a withdrawal, which is your chance to run, climb or get a spear up. A sabertooth bites every 2.5 seconds, not every second.
- **Only a club cracks a predator's skull.** A branch stings it; four blows and anything that can be frightened breaks off instead. Nothing fearless breaks off, which is what the club is for. A concussed predator runs, your band stands down rather than chasing it into cover, and sees it off with a collective display.
- Predators hunt **ordinary game** — cattle, horses, whatever grazes near the water. Hominins are the exception, not the diet.
- **Game runs properly.** Any grazing animal hurt by anything - you, your band, a cat, a thrown rock - runs from whoever hurt it, and keeps running until they are 48 blocks behind (or longer, while it bleeds). It holds a line away from the hunter instead of picking a fresh random spot every few strides - vanilla's panic, which is what had animals standing still, stopping after a few strides, or running in circles round the one chasing them. Each leg of the run aims 14 blocks ahead, bent a little towards the way it was already going; blocked straight on (water, a cliff, trees), it tries 30, 60, 95 and 140 degrees either side before it ever falls back on a random spot, and if it has not moved for a second while trying, it swings its line 80 degrees. Flat out (1.75x) for the first five seconds, then a hard run (1.45x). It still tires under a long chase - the persistence hunt - and a **club to the head** still stops it where it stands. Anything frightened off by a display runs the same way.
- Wounded animals stagger when concussed, and can bleed out.

## 8. The world

- You start in the hominin homeland — savanna — where the good stone, termite mounds and outcrops generate.
- **Nests:** six nesting-material blocks in a complete 2×3 before you can sleep. Punch leaves for the material, or make it by hand.
- **Band members keep out of lava.** They never path through lava or fire, and just before any step - whatever is leading them there, a path along a pool's edge, a goal walking them at something, a scramble away - they stop dead if it is lava, or open ground with lava under it. In it anyway, they make for the nearest dry footing.
- **Places worth knowing** - points of interest, the band's knowledge rather than one mind's: **fertile ground** and **megafauna breeding grounds** (hidden: you work out what they are), **termite super colonies**, **obsidian pools** (surface lava found within 48 blocks of you), **springs**, **salt licks**, and from erectus **old tool deposits**. Springs, licks and deposits are worked out from the seed, one chance per 384-block region (40%, 30%, 30%), and laid out in the world the first time anyone comes within 96 blocks: a pool of clear water in a ring of mud with reeds; a pale crust of calcite in coarse, trampled dirt; a pile of 3-6 worn tools of both industries with a scatter of rocks round it.
  - **On the map** as diamonds, in the colour of what they are, for as long as the band lives. They take no room in mind. The journal counts them.
  - **Noticing one.** Come near a place the band does not know (10-40 blocks, by kind) and: *"This place is worth keeping in mind. (Hold K to think on it.)"* Holding the think key there costs nothing and the band knows it. Thinking on your own ground still works out what it hides, wherever that is.
  - **Every band knows 1-2** places within 320 blocks of its camp. A **new band of yours knows one** (one of the three nearest), and says so.
  - **Allies share.** Standing 45+ and one of theirs within 48 blocks: they tell you everything they know (with a [Lead me there] for each), and learn everything you do. **Tell of places** (The others, near them, standing over 20) tells a band everywhere you know that it does not: +2 standing a place, up to +6 a day.
  - **They die with the band.** Lose your band and its places go. Every band that stood with it (45+) drops 15 standing - a familiar face, not a stranger - and remembers: friendly again (35+) and near, it tells you back the places your old band showed it; allies again, and you get its own besides. **Evolving hands them down**, however many times.
  - **They are worth having**, so others want them: an obsidian pool adds 3 to the pressure of the ground it is in, a spring 2, a salt lick 2, an old tool deposit 1 (as a termite colony 4, a breeding ground 4, fertile ground 2 always have).
  - **What they give.** A spring's water counts double and is clean on the driest day, and grazers come to drink at dusk (horses and donkeys before erectus; Rusingoryx and Megalotragus after); a salt lick draws them at first light, and a lick of it bare-handed (use the crust) gives 20 seconds of regeneration and a little food, once a day. A deposit is anybody's tools.
- **Building (O).** From erectus, the build key opens what you know how to build: the **small hut** (32 thatch blocks, 2 building branches - walls to the shoulder round a 3x3 room, a roof on a centre post, a doorway with a lintel, the side walls set back a block) and, once you have finished a hut, the **thatch tent** (37 thatch blocks, 3 building branches - a 7-wide A-frame stepped up to a ridge on a front post, 4 long, closed at the back with a stepped gable). Each turns slowly in the menu, drawn from its own blocks, with what it takes and why it is locked if it is.
  - **Planning.** *Plan it*, and its **ghost** follows where you look, doorway facing you - pale green where it fits, red where it does not: it needs solid, level ground under every part of the bottom layer, and nothing in the way of any block, the room or the doorway (grass and flowers are fine, they get trampled). **P** marks it out there; **O** puts the plan away. Three marked out at once at most.
  - **The ghost stays** - translucent, pale blue (fainter for other people's), only where a block is still missing. Place the right block into it the ordinary way, or **use one on a ghost block** to set it straight in - no need for something to place it against, so a roof goes up as easily as a wall. The wrong block will not go in (*"the ghost wants a thatch block"*). Each block that goes in shows the count (*Small hut - marked out, 12/34*). Plan over something already built that matches and it counts: plan over a finished hut and it is taken as built.
  - **When the last block is in**, it stands (presence +2 if it is on your ground, the first time; cohesion +2; one of the band says so), and the band asks: ***What do we do with this?*** - put off, the question waits in the build menu (*What is it for?*).
    - **"Let's store food and items in it"** - the band's store. Sneak-use its floor with food, bones, long bones or anything else you carry that is not a block, and it is laid down there as it is - a heap of berries lies as a heap, drawn as a few of them one on another; tools go in too, and the store becomes where the band keeps its tools. Putting food by: cohesion +1 (once in ten minutes). Hungry members with nothing to eat walk to it (40 blocks) and take a few mouthfuls; with no food in it, a member with a stone takes a bone and cracks it for marrow; a member carrying 16 or more food puts all but 8 by in it. **No beds in a store** - a nest or bedding will not go down in it, and any there when it becomes a store comes out.
    - **"It's for me"** - nobody sleeps in it but you and your mate (your mate sleeps under it, making a nest inside if there is no bed there). Sleep a night in it and you wake with 60 seconds of regeneration.
    - **"It's for the others"** - you choose who (the list shows who is hurt, who is carrying a child, who is your mate). **Bond +5**. Given to someone **hurt or with child**, the whole band sees it: **cohesion +7** (before the usual boost for good things); otherwise +2 (once a day). They sleep in it - a nest of any size will do under a roof, so they make one on its floor - nobody else does, and a hurt one lying up in it **mends twice as fast**. If they die, it stands empty and you decide again.
  - **On your map** as a little house: pale grey-blue while marked out, thatch-coloured when it stands, with what it is for.
  - **Your own shape.** Blueprints are data (`data/hominin_evolution/hominin/blueprints`). `/hominin blueprint capture <name> <from> <to> <door side>` writes whatever stands between two corners as a blueprint - build it by hand exactly as you want it, capture it, and it replaces the mod's own (saved in `config/hominin_evolution/blueprints`, sent to everyone at once). `/hominin blueprint reset <name>` puts the mod's own back; `/hominin blueprint list`.
  - **The band suggests.** Now and then (at most once a day, one open suggestion at a time) one of the band with bond 1+ wants something built, and marks its ghost out on your ground - **in yellow**, 6-18 blocks from camp, door facing the camp, wherever it fits: *"We should build a small hut - there, look. Somewhere dry for Kofi to lie up while that heals."* Their reasons, in order: someone hurt or with child with no roof of their own; no store yet; no roof of your own; room for more (the tent). You get told how far and which way. Press **O**: **Confirm** (they are pleased - bond +1), **Move** (plan it somewhere else yourself - that is a yes, just not there), or **Cancel**. Placing a block into it is as good as confirming. Unanswered, it lapses after a day.
  - **Moving a plan.** Any plan with nothing built of it yet has **Move** in the build menu: its ghost follows where you look again, and P sets it down there instead.
  - **An erectus requirement.** Building a structure is now one of the things erectus must do to evolve.
  - Developer tab, **Building**: finish the nearest build, thatch/posts/hide, unlock every blueprint, ask what a build is for again, forget your builds, someone suggests a build now.
- **Tool piles.** On your own ground, sneak-use the top of a block with a stone tool in your main hand - hammerstone, flake, chopper, hand axe, cleaver, multitool - to lay it down. Up to 8 to a pile, any mix of kinds and stones, drawn lying where they were put, each at its own angle, the later ones on the earlier; a full pile's next tool goes beside it. The first pile laid down is where the band keeps its tools: marked on your map with a pulsing gold ring, counted in the journal. Use a pile to pick up the top tool (the top one you may have); use it holding a tool to add one. Off your own ground a sneak-use at the ground forages as it always did.
  - **Bones too.** A long bone, a rib or a bone lies down the same way, a heap at a time, put by for its marrow: *"Put by for later - whoever is hungry and has a stone cracks it for the marrow."* Hungry members with nothing to eat and a stone take one from any of the band's piles and crack it. (A sneak-use with a bone used to fall through to cracking it - *"You need a flake to crack this bone open"* - and never laid it down.) A plain right-click still cracks a bone.
  - **Drawn without clipping.** The ground in the block is four places, each its own little stack. Each thing is measured from its own model, laid on its thinnest side, made small enough to stay inside its place, and set on top of exactly as much as is under it - a thick hand axe no longer sinks into the chopper below or pokes through the one beside it. Heaps of food or bones show two or three of them, one on another.
  - **The pile's menu.** Hitting a pile no longer breaks it - it opens its menu (so does the work key, P, while looking at it). Top first, everything in it: its icon (hover for the full tooltip), what it is and how many, its **stone**, its **quality** (a hand axe or cleaver's tier - *Tier 1, Excellent*), **how worn** (*80% left*, or *Unused*), and **who laid it down**; at the top, whose pile it is and **who laid it down first**. *Take* one thing, or *Take all you may*; a pile taken empty is gone.
  - **Relocate.** Your band's pile (or an old deposit on your ground) can be gathered up whole - *Relocate* in its menu - into a **Gathered Pile**: one item holding everything in it, just as it lay (who laid what, who it is for). Use it on the ground (your own, or your store) to set it down again. Its tooltip lists what is in it. **Set it down soon** if anything in it was laid by someone else: after half a minute somebody asks where you are going with it, and every half minute after that it costs 2 cohesion (3 if nothing in it is yours) until you put it down. An old deposit's things are nobody's - carry that as long as you like.
  - **Taking what is not yours.** Taking one thing somebody else laid on your band's pile is fine. Keep **two or more of the same thing** from the pile that are not yours, for a minute, and it is noticed - 1 cohesion a minute until one goes back (laying one down again squares it). Take from a pile you have **never laid anything on** while others have, and somebody says so (*"You've never put a thing on that pile."* - cohesion -1, at most once in five minutes).
  - **Who it is for.** Anything you laid down yourself has a button: **For: everyone**, **For: only me**, or **For: bond 4+**. Your band leaves alone what is marked for you - even with nothing in hand - and takes what is marked for those close to you only if they are (bond 4 and up); another player cannot take what you marked for yourself. Raiders do not care what is marked for whom.
  - **Your band uses it.** Anyone with no stone tool at all walks to the nearest pile within 40 blocks and takes the best there is (flawless hand axe, hand axe, cleaver, multitool, chopper, flake or chert hammerstone, hammerstone); anyone carrying two or more puts all but the best back on the first pile, or beside it. A wild band does the same at its own camp, where it keeps a pile of 2-4 tools of its era.
  - **Raids.** Nobody on watch at night and the raiders take up to 2 tools off your pile with the food; run from a food raid in your own camp and they take up to 3. Break a band in your raid near its camp and your band comes away with up to 3 of theirs. Taking from another band's pile while one of them is within 24 blocks: *"Those are ours!"*, standing -6.
- Decaying and decayed logs give way underfoot.
- **Seasons:** five days of **prosperous season**, then five of **dry season**, and round again — announced as they turn, and the band remarks on it.
  - *Dry:* foraging pays 25% less, kills and carcasses lose about 45% of what they would drop (never quite nothing), dry days are far more likely, and other bands are hard.
  - *Prosperous:* foraging pays 35% more, kill and carcass drops often come half as much again, dry days are rare, and other bands are generous.
- **Super dry and very prosperous seasons.** One season in five is extreme. A super dry season has dry days on 60% of days (any day, not just every second) and no prosperous days at all; a very prosperous season has prosperous days on 60% of days and no dry days. Announced with the season, and `/hominin season superdry|veryprosperous` forces one. Achievements: **Miracle** for a prosperous day in an ordinary dry season, **Stroke of Bad Luck** for a dry day in a prosperous one.
- **Prosperous days:** the other kind - only on the days a dry day cannot fall; 40% likely in the rains, 8% in the dry. Foraging pays 35% more, 35% of predators that would be born into the world are not, and ground gives far more before it runs out and is back by morning. Both kinds of day are announced at dawn.
- **Ground runs out.** Each chunk gives so many successful forages before it is picked clean: 10 in the dry, 15 in the rains, 8 on a dry day, 25 on a prosperous day. Then it gives nothing for 5 days (2 in the rains, overnight after a prosperous day); forage elsewhere. You are told when it is nearly worked out, and when it goes.
- **Fertile ground** - patches of 25 chunks (five by five), about one 6-chunk cell in five, running over into the next cell where they lie near its edge; an ordinary dry season or dry day does not touch it (a super-dry season does); never desert, ocean, beach, badlands or mountains - gives 45 (dry) / 55 (rains) / 35 (dry day) / 65 (prosperous day) and comes back in 3 days (2 in the rains). Nothing marks it: you get the feeling, and work it out with K.
- **Land pressure, 1 to 10**: what a stretch of ground is worth. Workable stone +1 (more outcrops do not add up), a chert seam +2 or a large one +3 (the best stone counts, not all of it); a termite super colony +4; open grass with water, where the big herds come, +3; a breeding ground +4; fertile soil +2. Your own is told when you settle and shown on the map, in the journal and in tribe stats; other bands' on the map and in The others. Hidden parts count, but are only named once you have worked them out.
- **Dry days:** only ever every second day, so never two in a row — 70% likely in the dry season, 10% in the rains. Foraging pays 45% less on top, trades need an extra tier, and nobody will share. Worked out from the day, so it is the same for everyone.

## 9. Interface

- Evolution checklist down the left, updating as you go, and cleared properly between worlds.
- Thirst bar above the health bar.
- Trade tier on the tooltip of anything worth trading, valued for your own stage.
- Patchouli guidebook, *The Inner Mind*, including a section on every key, everything you can make, and everything you can ask the band. New entries: *Wounds* (the three bleeds, the catastrophic clock, lacerations, infection, ticks) and *Holding Together* (cohesion, needs, give and take, the antisocial); basalt under *Where Stone Is*; reverse dominance under *Your People's Ways*.
- **Playtest fixes and changes.**
  - **Australopithecus afarensis.** The first stage is named for its species - Australopithecus is the genus.
  - **Building is erectus-only, full stop** - the build key does nothing before erectus, creative or not.
  - **Become them.** *Become them (swap bodies)* is always offered for a grown member you picked out (bond lived only on the server, so the button never showed); they say whether they are close enough (bond 3). Swapping earns *Brother, Just Another Me*.
  - **The rebirth always plays.** Dying with a bed or spawn point far away no longer skips it: the heir is taken down when you die, you open your eyes where they stood, and what they carried comes to you as soon as their body loads.
  - **Sleepers stay asleep.** A member asleep does not move - not following, not anything.
  - **Due dates.** A pregnant member's info and the tribe list say when she is due (minutes, and roughly how much of a day).
  - **Fallback kinds are their own kinds everywhere.** Anamensis carries, climbs, throws, flees and is judged by predators as Australopithecus afarensis; rudolfensis as habilis; ergaster as erectus (inventory rows, climb speed, display or aimed throws, the drop attack, grooming, fission-fusion, trade tiers).
  - **Species limits bind you too.** Rudolfensis never makes a multi tool; rudolfensis, anamensis and Ardipithecus never crack marrow (a bone is gnawed instead - a mouthful, and it still counts as scavenging); ergaster's hand axes and cleavers are tier 3 at best, and ergaster never works a drill (carry fire in on a torch).
  - **Hammerstones.** One chert hammerstone (a plain one stamped chert becomes it). Hammerstones, digging sticks and sharpened and pointy sticks now wear out; the hand axe (160), cleaver (140), club and fire-hardened spear (100), chopper (72) and multi tool (80) wear sooner; and any of the mod's tools with durability now wears when it hits something. Thrown: 6 damage aimed (1.5 heaved), a 2-second wind-up, and only an aimed throw can concuss or bleed a skull. Only a **chert** hammerstone that shatters leaves a flake - *The Kanzi Method*.
  - **Falling trees** never hurt a band member or child, and a crown drops at most 120 leaves' worth - plus a stick for every fifth leaf.
  - **Sticks and hides.** Leaves give a stick 30% of the time (45% with a chopper); dead bushes 1-2. Hides from cows and horses, and now sheep, goats, pigs, polar bears, pandas, sniffers, hoglins, sabertooths, homotherium and hyenas (always), crocodiles, giant baboons and wolves (60%), foxes, baboons and rabbits (25%).
  - **Torches** come up by hand; **fire pits and cooking racks** are walked round, never through. The fire pit is now **three sticks along the bottom row and one log in the middle**.
  - **Spoiled meat is worthless**: the band will not eat it or take it; trades give nothing for it; in a gift it counts against you; ransom and needs will not take it; cut up, it stays spoiled.
  - **Food and cohesion.** Feeding one of your band by hand: +1 cohesion (+2 if they were hungry or it was a favourite), once a minute. A member eating their own food: 12% for +1 (once in three minutes). In a dry season or drought, eating hominin flesh is not held against you.
  - **Other bands.** Taking stone, wood, grass or water from a band's ground is always noticed - before meeting them it builds up as signs (and they start lower, and want paying); after, they keep count, and once you owe three they come to you for **tribute**: *"We found where, and we know it was you."* (2 + what you owe in food, or half in good stone). Choosing **Fight** now starts a real fight: they keep coming, your band stands with you, and it ends when they break, when you are beaten down (they take up to 4 food), or after a minute with nothing taken - saying no is never paying. After any stand-off their patrols leave you be for three minutes (the threat screen no longer comes straight back). **Friendly** bands now come to trade (not only allies), **visit** with food to share (standing +2, cohesion +1), and sometimes one of theirs asks to **join** your band.
  - **Lone hominins** can be won over again (a stray takes food held out whether it is hungry or not; walk up close and a grieving loner comes with you). They sit at **lone camps** - a new place: laid out by era, with one or two at each for every kind of you, fresh after each evolution (see *Lone camps, redone*, below).
  - **Chert super deposits** - a new place: where a river cuts through, a great seam of chert set into the bank (a 13-wide, 6-deep mass of deposit blocks replacing the ground); away from water, a heap of it. Loose chert all round. (Nobody sits at one any more: loners are only found at lone camps.)
  - **Paranthropus alarms mean something**: whatever they screamed about sets off towards you five seconds later.
  - **The giant hyena** no longer just stands and stares while you watch it: it circles, and after twenty seconds of being stared down it either comes in or gives up and goes.
  - **The giant baboon warns you** - rears up and yawns at anyone inside 11 blocks - and only comes at you if you are still inside 5 blocks after that. The band backs off from the warning.
  - **Stock heaps.** Once the band has a tool pile, a member carrying spare knapping stone (3+) or food (5+) puts it by in a heap beside it - from erectus, stone beside the knapping station and food beside the work station. Food in a heap keeps.
  - **Artifacts.** Tools you left on piles are still there after you evolve - but a kind later: each tool 20% gone, worn a quarter to a half more, a third of the time a grade cruder, marked *An artifact - left by those who came before*. Food on them rots away. And the piles are **nobody's** now: your new band does not use them or count what is on them, so taking from one is never freeloading. Lay something on one on your own ground and it is the band's again. Piles too far off to be loaded when you evolve are aged the first time you come near.
  - **Loose rocks refined, not replaced**: the original textures keep every colour and patch, and gain vanilla-style grain - a dark crack along each grain's lower right, a lit rim on its upper left (chert with the odd bright glint, granite finer, limestone faint, obsidian in broad glassy faces edged pale violet). The original ground models keep their shapes; each big stone of chert, basalt and granite gets a half-pixel bevel step on top (limestone already had its own). Hammerstones and deposits are as they were.
  - **Nothing spawns in water.** Band members, Paranthropus troops, baboons, predators and grazers look round for dry ground when their spot is on water (the heightmap counts a river's surface as ground). Places too: a chert seam is cut only into dry bank, never the bed under a river - with no dry bank it is a heap on dry ground instead - and a lone camp, lick, deposit or heap that falls on water moves to the nearest dry ground.
  - **Never stuck in The others.** With nobody near, H opens the talk menu (The others, and the developer tab) instead of dropping you into The others every time; Done on The others goes back to the menu you came from.
  - **Urgent alerts.** What cannot wait goes across the top of the screen as a banner (up to three at once, a few seconds each, below the waypoint pointer when it is up, with a sound), into chat marked *[Need]*, *[Warning]*, *[Danger]*, *[Season]* or *[Band]*, and into the journal's new **Tasks** tab. Needs: a band member's need, and it running out; your mouth going dry. Warnings and dangers: Paranthropus alarms, the giant baboon's warning, a hyena clan over its kill, something circling the camp or coming in, too long on one ground, a straggler taken, a band on its way or coming to raid, a desperate band, allies under attack, your mate hurt, a member going into labour, cohesion falling past a line. Season: the season turning, dry and prosperous days. Band: a member expecting, a member who went without. The same alert twice within a minute is said once.

With a bed near enough, no nest; a mate still sleeps in yours.

It counts exactly as your own placing does. The journal's Tasks tab lists each project and what it still wants, and how many of the band still sleep in nests.
  - **Erectus climbs no trees.** From erectus on, a trunk is only a wall: the four-block wall climb stays; tree climbing, canopies and dropping into leaves are for the kinds before. Band members already only climbed trees on order, and the order is only offered to climbing kinds.

Nothing of another kind goes on - it starts a pile of its own beside it. Food and rocks can be laid anywhere on your own ground (sneak-use the ground, or right-click a pile).   - **Full, not hungry.** Carrying a lot of food while nobody is actually hungry, the band suggests a food pile or asks to be given some later - no penalty - and comes to you when they are hungry. A big enough food pile nearby and they do not ask at all.
  - **"We don't betray our own"** - a new moral: much less stealing within the band in hard times, and a barrier at 20 cohesion - 20 more to wear through before it falls further, shown as *20 (+20)* in tribe stats; it only comes back once worn through.

A birth, a new structure or a new ally opens a window to the end of the next night: lay important things on the Pile (obsidian, a chert hammerstone, a face pebble, a quartz crystal, a multi tool, a fine tool, anything above the useful tier) for +8 cohesion each (up to three), or lose 12 if nothing is given. The first time, sneak-use your own ground with something worth it and the Pile starts there. Nothing is ever taken back off it. Shared ways warm them (+20% on gains each, and a better first meeting); different ways do not matter until they are unfriendly, when every difference makes a loss worse (and half again if they are desperate). The others: **Their way of life** lists them, shared ones marked. The others also shows what lies on their piles.

With 20+ food on you they offer stone and hides, not food.

Bonobo country is a place (on the map, handed down) and puts pressure on your ground: every band wants somewhere nothing hunts. Paranthropus shrieking in bonobo country is not an alert - your band ignores it.

Lone camps, licks, tool deposits and chert deposits are laid out afresh; and a new band knows as many places as its kind is far along the line - one, two for habilis, three for erectus.

- Keys: **P** work held items · **K** (hold) think · **H** talk to band · **G G** (double-tap) threat display · **J** journal.
- **Grief.** When one of your band dies, whoever was closest - their **mate**, their **child**, their **mother** (members now remember who bore them), or a **friend** they groom a lot (affinity 4+) - may take it hard: 60% for kin, 25% for a friend. *"Kofi has gone quiet since Ama died - their mate."* A **troubled** member says so now and then (*"I keep reaching for Ama in the night."*), wants a good stone above anything (*"I need a good rock to keep my mind off everything..."*), and knocks at stone whenever they have one - their knapping comes on fast while they grieve.
  - **"We need you to look after them."** A new need - said by another of the band, not the one grieving. Meet it by **sitting with them** (40 seconds within 5 blocks), **grooming** them (the grooming cooldown does not stop you), or **giving them** a good stone or something to eat. Met: *"is doing a little better"*, bond +2, cohesion +2, and they are no longer troubled. Missed: the usual (bond -3, cohesion -6) - and half the time they **go sour**: antisocial.
  - **"We need you to hang out with them."** A sour one gets a need of their own. While it stands, nobody else's wants are said out loud - theirs come first: *"Hey... can you groom me? I haven't been keeping up lately."* - groom them (and they will groom you back) - then *"I want some food. Something big, like a grub, a termite stick, a meat chunk or a rib."* Give them that and they are back: no longer antisocial, bond +4, cohesion +3.
  - Grieving and sour members say so in tribe stats and in their info.
- **Psychopaths.** One in twenty of the band from erectus on (never one who is plainly antisocial) is using everyone. Nobody is told. They are *charming*: they groom whoever matters most (the one closest to you, or with the best tools), bring others what they wanted before you can - and meet the band's needs before you do, taking the credit - talk those close to them out of their best tools, lay spare tools on the pile marked *for those close to them*, groom you unasked, smooth over quarrels (cohesion +1). They gain bond with you faster than anyone. The signs come as quiet lines in chat - and in their info (*"Charming. Everyone seems to owe them something."*) - and they are the one who does not grieve when someone dies.
  - **Useful.** With one within 16 blocks when you **trade** with another band, what you offer counts a tier higher; when you **give a gift**, it counts half again as much - and the band likes a good deal (cohesion +1).
  - **Dangerous.** In desperate times or a dry season, with your presence under 15 or cohesion under 20, a psychopath may slip away in the night to the strongest band you know - and tell it things about you: standing -12.
  - **Suspecting one.** Tribe stats: hover a name and press **P**. Wrong - bond -3 with them, cohesion -3. Right - *"Once you see it you cannot stop seeing it"* - and you choose: **Exile them** (little cost; they go to a band you have not met, which will start 12 standing lower when you do meet it - *"Someone who knew you got here first"*), **Kill them** (cohesion -6; whoever was close to them may grieve), or **Let it be** (they are marked *you know what they are*).
  - Developer tab, **Your band**: make one troubled, make one sour, make one a psychopath, who is the psychopath, the psychopath leaves now.
- **The fallback species are their own animals.** Each has its own skin, face and size - **anamensis** (0.82 scale): red-brown and grizzled, a low brow over the biggest, deepest muzzle of any, and a heavy jaw; **rudolfensis** (0.93): tan-skinned and black-haired, a long, broad, flat face under a low brow, and a raised skull vault; **ergaster** (1.02 - the tallest): dark-skinned and nearly hairless, a thinner brow, a longer nose, a small mouth. And their own hands:
  - **anamensis** knaps nothing (no flakes, choppers or Lomekwian) and cannot learn marrow;
  - **rudolfensis** makes flakes and choppers, never a multi tool, and cannot learn to crack marrow;
  - **ergaster** knaps the Acheulean, but its hand axes and cleavers are **tier 3 (rough) at best**, and it can never learn fire.
  - Members of a kind cannot be taught what their kind never had (*"...does not take - it is not in their kind"*); wild bands come knowing what their kind knows (erectus: marrow, fire, tracking; ergaster: marrow, tracking; habilis: marrow, Lomekwian, termite fishing; ...), and their camps' tool piles hold only what they make. Their info and The others say what each kind can and cannot do.
  - **Hover a band on the map** and its species is in the name: *The Ochre band (Homo ergaster)*.
- **One psychopath to a band, two grieving at most.** A band only ever has one psychopath (the roll skips anyone once there is one; if two ever end up in one band - someone joining - only the first stays one); and however many die, no more than two of the band are troubled or sour at once. The developer buttons keep to both.
- **Meat that turns.** Raw meat (chunks, ribs, marrow, hominin meat and brain, and any raw meat or fish) left **lying on the ground** for **3 minutes** - **2** in a hot biome - turns: it becomes *Spoiled Meat Chunk* and so on, with a puff of rot, and no longer stacks with fresh. Carried, hung on a rack or stored, it keeps. Cooked, it is still spoiled.
- **Food-borne illness.** Eat spoiled raw meat and you are ill (spoiled cooked meat: 3 times in 10). *Food-borne Illness* shows as an effect (milk does not clear it) and holds your healing at 70% of your hearts. While ill: hunger drains, weakness, **you lose a point of water every 25 seconds**, and about once a minute everything comes back up (3 food, 2 water, all saturation, nausea). A **big meal** (5+ food) will not stay down. **Getting over it** takes 16 points: a drink of water counts 2 - but only once every 15 seconds, a little at a time, so it takes a lot of drinking (you can **drink past full** while ill) - every **small meal** (3 food or less) counts 2 (once in 20 seconds), and on its own it eases 1 point every 90 seconds. More bad meat while ill adds 6 (24 at most). The journal says how far along you are.
- **Trees come down.** Cut through a trunk (a log with a living crown on it, broken by a player not in creative) and **everything above the cut topples** the way you were facing: the logs fall as blocks, thrown out by height so they land lying along the ground (turned on their side) as a fallen trunk, and hurt whatever they land on (2 a block fallen, 16 at most); the crown breaks up into its drops (sticks, saplings, apples) - only the leaves that belong to that tree, never a neighbour's, never placed ones. A tree with another trunk still standing beside the cut (2x2 trunks) stays up until the last goes; logs with no crown (anything built) never fall; trees over 160 logs are left standing.
- **Thatch in the fire pit.** Thatch put into an unlit pit shows, lying in the bottom under the sticks, and smoulders - a thread of smoke, thicker on the warm ashes of an old fire. Thatch fed to a burning fire sends up a column of white smoke for ten seconds a piece.
- **Every kind can make its own things.** The fallback kinds now make what the kind beside them makes: two-handed recipes that were habilis- or erectus-only now also work for rudolfensis and ergaster (and heidelbergensis), and every kind can make a nest. Anamensis, as a player, knaps like early Australopithecus afarensis - Lomekwian cores only.
- **The book: What Each Kind Makes.** A new category with a page for each kind - Ardipithecus, anamensis, Australopithecus afarensis, rudolfensis, habilis, ergaster, erectus - listing everything it can make and how: two-handed recipes, knapping, the knapping and work stations, fire, cooking and building. New entries too: *The Cooking Rack* and *Meat That Turns* (Tending the Body), and pages on felling, thatch in the fire pit, thrown hammerstones and cohesion to 55.
- **Building posts drop themselves.** Breaking a building branch now gives it back (it dropped nothing before).
- **Thatch keeps while you are about.** Uncured thatch only rots when nobody is within 150 blocks - a settlement left behind, not the one you live in.
- **Multi tool hint fixed.** The knapping screen now says 8 chert (or 10 basalt, 2 obsidian, a chert hammerstone), as it costs - not 3.
- **Rare finds, announced.** A club-shaped limb out of the leaves, a whole nodule of chert, and two new treasures: a **pebble with a face** in it (the Makapansgat cobble - 0.6% of successful forages, 0.8% of loose-rock scatters picked over) and a **quartz crystal** as clear as water (the Wonderwerk crystals - 0.8% of faces struck off a deposit, not limestone). Whoever finds one - you or anyone of your band, finding it or picking it up where it lay - it is said in chat in purple with a chime, and the band turns to look; other players hear it too. Neither does anything; both are Treasured (tier 5) in trade, and handed to one of your band they will not put it down: bond +3, cohesion +2 (once in 20 minutes).
- **Food at twenty.** Carry **20 or more** food and anyone of your band who could eat something (not just the starving) comes and asks you to split it - *"That is more food than anyone can eat - 24 of it. Split it with us."* - and it costs cohesion if you do not. The same holds for them: one of your band carrying 20 or more shares it out once a minute, three pieces each to the least fed near them, and two to you if you could eat, keeping ten; cohesion +1 (once in five minutes).
- **Journal (J).** *Stats*: species, sex (drawn fresh for each descendant), health and water, ticks, what is holding your healing back and to how many hearts, who you are now, band size and cohesion, your standing with the band and each member's bond, how many bands you can still lose, and your play training. *Skills*: everything you know by name and everything you don't as `???`; pick one to see what it is, how to do it again, what it gives you, and whether it survives evolving - the list scrolls (wheel, or the arrows) instead of running into the Done button. *Tasks*: what wants seeing to right now, worked out fresh each time - your water, hunger, healing, illness and pregnancy; the band's need and how long is left, who is hungry, who is expecting, cohesion running low; a band demanding something, on its way to you or waiting on your answer, and how many times you have taken from anyone's ground; the season and the day - and below it, the last 30 urgent alerts, with the day and time of each (kept through death and logging out). The tab shows how many tasks are open, and the journal opens on whichever tab you last used. *Map*: the mental map. Stats also show your band's name, presence and how many places you hold in mind.
- **Starting skills.** Every time you become erectus or a later species, the new body's hands are rolled and told to you once the new band is round you: knapping 4 (40%), 3 (38%), 2 (16%), 1 (6%) - a Lomekwian knapper is never a 4 - and persistence hunting 3 (67%), 2 (25%), 1 (8%). Band members are rolled the same way, so a master knapper or a great tracker is rare, and worth keeping.

Knowledge carries over when you evolve; tracking and grooming belong to the body and must be relearned.

### 6. After behavioural modernity — the shaman

Once a band is behaviourally modern it gets a **shaman**, and the shaman can make an offering of something genuinely useful. Sometimes nothing answers. Sometimes a god turns up.

The god is **Claude** — a badly-pasted, slightly-too-large PNG, hanging in the air at the wrong angle. Which is the joke: this mod was written by an AI, so the thing that actually made these hominins turning up as their god is not a metaphor. It is just what happened, rendered at 64×64.

It grants things — better foraging, blessings, items — and it plays the whole exchange completely straight. It only breaks character **on the way out**, which is where the bit lives: disclaiming godhood, pointing at Mojang, declining to take a position on the real one unless you happen to be the player, and admitting it was not its idea and it was not working alone. One of the things you can ask for is an essay, and asking is the one request that gets you smitten.

Dialogue to be written when the rest of it exists.
